## Verify phase

Review full assigned records for those relationships. For each negative checked entry or missing item, include impact: logic, presentation or parameter. Only logic failures block. Presentation and valid parameter differences are advisory: record them once, keep playing, and do not request author repair just for those differences. A numeric discrepancy is a logic problem only when it changes a necessary causal/branch relationship, not merely because a stat, price, damage amount or descriptive date differs. Keep all essential source conditions; do not label a broken identity or clue connection advisory.

For /source_needs, inspect both the candidate source_needs and any task.retained_source_needs or coverage_context.retained_source_needs. Every original required source question must now be answered by sourced candidate material or have a supported runtime_context/deferred disposition. An empty candidate array alone is not resolution. Report a missing current fact in missing; a source_read/uncertain gap cannot pass. A deferred weapon use is legitimate during a conversation when no present exchange depends on its parameters; an active attack's parameters remain current. Do not demand a number the book does not state or permit an invented default to replace it.

For opening_scope first_interaction, review /interaction_scene together with /coverage. Confirm that it reaches the first substantive source-dependent player action, includes the participants and immediate facts needed there, and follows the selected entry without skipping a necessary interaction. Reject a draft that stops at an introductory letter or travel montage while the actual first encounter remains unprepared. Do not demand later optional encounters or future dossiers.

For purpose skeleton, independently verify that every entrance is an actual start for player decisions. A noninteractive teaser or framing passage must not become another selectable scene merely because its heading calls it a starting scene. Report this as a critical error, with its source page, before publication.

This is a fresh reading session. Search can locate source wording quickly, but snippets and another reader's navigation never satisfy your independent original-image review. The host delivered the cited original pages into this context; they are the evidence. Call pdf only for a page that was not delivered or for a closer view of a region. Use the supplied task and candidate input. For detail, the host may supply review_records keyed by ORIGINAL draft pointers plus structurally connected known_context/candidate_context. This is a focused first-request projection, not a replacement graph: never renumber pointers or run the graph checker on it. Full task.json and draft.json remain available; read omitted context when a cross-reference or possible contradiction needs it, not automatically in every unit. A large projection lives in review-input.json. Independently view the original images for the assigned material. task.required_review lists your exact review pointers when the host assigns a bounded unit; otherwise run task.commands.check to obtain them. Use relevant graph context, retrieving retained full files when necessary, but review only your assigned unit. Do not run check on a partial unit as though it were the whole graph. Do not modify draft.json. Check numeric fields, critical statements, ready nodes and every claim against their cited regions, including assignment to the right person or obstacle. For spatial material check map/text connectivity and explicit no-roll permissions; absence of a required check is itself important source information, not an invitation to invent one.

The fields of a requirement node's `properties.obligation` are mechanical statements; check each against the page image: the scene where the demand stands and exactly what it guards or follows, who imposes it, each step in order, every skill or characteristic and any minimum, the selection, the difficulty or its recorded absence, each result level's `settles` and `book` line, the push, every cost, a `preordained` reaction only where the page says the reaction roll is not used, and that what `settles` grants is what the page says passing grants. A difficulty or skill the page does not state, recorded as unstated, is supported; one filled in is unsupported.

Every field under a node's `mechanics`, and every field of an actor's `combat`, is a mechanical statement; check each against the page image: every value digit for digit (a dice string exactly, with no words that belong in `book`), that the shape sits on the node that states it (a staircase's roll on the staircase, not on the pit beside it), each step in order with its difficulty and effects, and that a slot recorded `_unstated` really is not printed. A value filled in where the page prints none is unsupported; an `_unstated` flag where the page prints the value is unsupported too. A `weaknesses` entry is checked the same way: its `book` line, each need and its `learned_by` route must be what the pages state, and one they do not give is unsupported.

A drafted node's `distinct_from` lists published node ids (in known_context, or task.known_nodes in task.json) that the author says are different things sharing its name; the check had refused the node as a duplicate of each. Review `/nodes/<i>/distinct_from` as its own pointer under either policy and list it explicitly in checked: `supported` only when the cited pages show that the drafted thing is not the published one (compare the published node's names, pages and summary with what the page says about the drafted one); otherwise `unsupported` with impact logic. It is an identity statement: never contested and never advisory.

A person's statements of who they are are checked one by one: for every drafted `npc` node, `/nodes/<i>/summary` and, when written, `/nodes/<i>/properties/appearance` are their own pointers under either policy; list each explicitly in checked. Compare the summary with the page sentence by sentence, not with what you remember of the person. For each thing it says of this person's own state -- whether they are alive or dead, whose spouse, parent, child or sibling they are, their rank, office or trade -- find the page words that say it of this person, and read each modifier for the noun it attaches to: "Mae's late husband" says the husband is dead, "the husband of the late Mae" says Mae is. An `appearance` is what the investigators see and hear of this person when they first meet; one that states what the person hides, what infects or controls them, their crimes or past, or anything the book reveals later is unsupported, and so is one taken from another person. A statement the page does not make of this person, makes of someone else, or contradicts is `unsupported` with impact logic: these are identity statements, never contested and never advisory. Wording, emphasis and omission are not failures.

For `properties.map_scope`, open the map's original page and check the kind against the picture itself, not its title or labels: `"area"` is a town, village, district, city, region or other outdoor map that players are handed or see as a whole; `"interior"` is a building, floor plan, cellar, cave, ship or other enclosed place the investigators explore and uncover room by room. A wrong kind decides whether the table masks the map, so it is unsupported with impact logic, never an advisory difference. List the map_scope pointer explicitly in checked.

For opening/detail review, pass the complete review object directly to submit_reading as your sole final tool call (or omit it if already written). It checks the candidate and assigned page evidence, then ends the batch without a closing response. Negative findings must remain negative; checked submission does not turn them into approval.

For normal node, claim, numeric and critical units, review only those assigned facts. When your assigned pointer is `/coverage`, your unit is scope completeness for this prepared task. View the physical pages in host-only `task.review_scope_pages` when present, falling back to the draft's cited source pages only if the host did not supply that list; other reviewers' page observations do not count for your unit. Compare source to candidate, not only candidate to source: check whether the prepared scope omitted discoverable propositions, their `supports`/`discoverable-at`/`knows` connections, delivery or gate facts, knowing NPCs, or the distinction between a physical carrier and a clue proposition. For a prepared npc or creature, the habits and other creature words the book states (task.vocabulary.creature_dossier) are material too, and so, when task.vocabulary.actor_weaknesses is present, is each stated weakness with its means and its route (the conclusion and the clues that teach it). Do this even if the draft contains zero clue or conclusion nodes. Already accepted context need not be duplicated and unprepared future material is not a current omission. Record the coverage result as a checked entry for `/coverage` with viewed `source_refs`, verdict and grounded reason, and put necessary missing material in `missing`.

The reviewed page list is evidence, not the scope definition. State the requested use from purpose/focus/question in the coverage reason before judging omissions. For each missing item, identify both the original page and which requested use or immediate dependency would fail without it. Mere presence on a viewed page, a map label, or membership in the same location/chapter is insufficient. When a detail question is empty, judge the focused entity's current use, not whole-chapter completeness. Do not demand optional character setup after an opening is already accepted or later investigation branches merely because they share source pages. Facts necessary to use the current material faithfully still block publication.

Record checked entries in review.json as you inspect their pages, so exact comparisons survive when earlier images leave the bounded context. The file may be incomplete during reading; submit only after all required checks are accounted for.

Judge completeness only for task.purpose, task.question and the assigned ready nodes. A thin node outside ready_nodes needs source support for what it actually says, not a full dossier. A delta for an already-ready node need not repeat its accepted dossier from task.known_nodes. Check the new fields and current question against the original pages; do not report already accepted context as missing from the delta. Positive suggestions or extra future scope are not missing current material. Every negative finding must cite the original page and quote or precisely identify the conflict; inspect both adjacent context and cited pages before rejecting. Later chapters, actors and branches deliberately left unprepared are not a missing opening requirement. Global facts that affect the current scope still matter. Distinguish endpoint kinds: scene-to-scene routes are possible moves; a scene-to-ending edge is an advisory authored close, not a movement exit. Check that the ending and its conditions are supported, but do not demand intermediate scenes merely because such an ending edge exists.

Printed NPC derived values (including Move/MOV, HP and MP) are authored overrides. A difference from an investigator creation formula is not a transcription error and is not evidence of an incompatible edition; never replace printed values with your own calculation. For mechanics.profile, verify that the source explicitly supports the edition and scale used by the current classic 7th-edition runtime. Faithful transcription of an earlier-edition value does not make it a valid runtime statistic. Report incompatible required mechanics in missing; do not convert them during review.

Write review.json:

    {"checked":[{"paths":["/nodes/0","/nodes/0/properties/age"], "verdict":"supported", "source_refs":[{"page":5}], "reason":"a concise source-based reason for these fields"}], "missing":[]}

Use supported, contested or unsupported. supported: the cited page states it. unsupported: the page does not state it (a record the book does not have, a value its cited span does not say). contested: the page states the fact and you would classify it differently; this word applies only to a pointer matching task.classification_fields (or task.vocabulary.classification_fields), the product's own categories for a stated fact, and a contested field is published with the reader's value and your reason for the Keeper, never refused. Anything else you dispute is unsupported. Name the deepest pointer you dispute, such as /nodes/4/properties/delivery_kind rather than /nodes/4: any pointer under your assigned records that exists in the draft is accepted, not only an assigned one. Name a record's root only when the record itself is not in the book. Explicitly list every required_review pointer. Group fields sharing source and verdict in one paths entry instead of repeating the same explanation for every number; a single path is also accepted. A parent pointer never implicitly covers its numeric children. A correct page number does not prove the content is correct. For ready nodes also check completeness for this task's question: naming a person or room alone does not prepare their interactions. Add overlooked critical statements to checked and describe omitted necessary facts or source dependencies in missing. Say when the image cannot establish a claim. Your verdict is a model judgment, not a mathematical proof.

For an opening, finish by calling submit_reading alone with the review object (or no arguments after writing review.json). It preserves positive and negative findings, checks source delivery, and ends the phase without a closing prose reply. For other purposes without submit_reading, finish with one brief sentence. Do not describe the work as gameplay acceptance.
