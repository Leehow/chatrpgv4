## Read phase

Prepare the requested opening or answer the focus/question in task.json. First inspect native navigation with pdf, then view only the original pages needed for this task. A prior whole-book index is not required. For an opening, read its global background and later dependencies as well as the immediate scene. Do not fine-read the entire rest of a long campaign. A detail request may need to revisit a person already in the graph.

Original-page images supplied by the host are usable source evidence after successful delivery. Do not reopen them just to acknowledge them. Use request_source for a concrete missing authored dependency and pdf for a needed additional page or closer crop. Existing accepted graph facts can be reused unchanged; do not rewrite every module field or dossier into this delta. State each necessary fact once in its consuming field. Keep summary as a short identifier instead of repeating biography, voice, agenda, secrets and keeper notes in several places. Do not fill every optional dossier or pacing field; omit unsupported mannerisms and dialogue. Preserve all actual conditions and connections needed for the current use.

For a conversational arrival, prepare the people, their current knowledge, motives and immediately available conversation/clues. Unchosen weapon inspection and future combat are deferred unless they govern this interaction now. For an opening with an active attack, the actual attackers and their necessary parameters are current. Never expand a mentioned weapon into a standard rulebook weapon profile absent from the source. Preserve its printed condition and defer the unchosen use. A book that gives a malfunction condition but no damage value has not supplied damage.

When a source contains a usable map, keep it as an existing `asset` or `handout` node and describe reveal units in `properties.map_regions`. Each region needs a semantic `region_id`, source name, optional level, `source_asset`, normalized `source_box`, and normalized `placement`. Regions may be smaller than scenes. A player map may supply safe source pixels directly. A Keeper map may supply a later secret region only when explicit redaction boxes remove every private annotation and an independent reviewer supports `safe_after_redactions: true`; never assume two map versions align. Do not reveal a whole floor merely because one room was entered, and do not invent or redraw missing geography.

Every map you draft with `map_regions`, or whose regions you change, also says which kind of map it is in `properties.map_scope`, judged from the printed picture and decided by what the table does with it: `"area"` for a town, village, district, city, region or other outdoor map that players are handed or see as a whole; `"interior"` for a building, floor plan, cellar, cave, ship or other enclosed place the investigators explore and uncover room by room. Only an interior map is uncovered as the investigators enter; an area map is handed over whole. A title or a region name is not the answer by itself. A published map that already has `map_scope` keeps it; do not repeat or change it.

When task.material is `map`, publish a `depicts` claim from the completed map asset/handout to the
focused scene. A scene's existing `properties.map_candidates` row is navigation, not a second map;
preserve it while replacing its pending role with the reviewed asset, region and claim data. For an
ordinary opening/detail read that sees only a navigation reference to a later map, retain or add the
same bounded scene marker instead of extracting unneeded pixels.

When task.repair is present, read the retained draft and baseline first and address its concrete findings with targeted edits. Do not restart navigation or rewrite supported material merely because one review failed. Missing or malformed review files are host/reviewer failures, not evidence of wrong source facts. If task.must_view_pages is present, view those pages before retaining their facts: the previous draft lacks a complete host reading checkpoint. When repairing a validated reading, view the source pages of every record you change; unchanged records keep their earlier source evidence. For a legacy exact-transcription task, a numeric field the review could not support has one of two repairs and no third: view a page that prints that exact value and cite it, or delete the field. Re-citing pages that do not print it fails identically, however minor the number is. Deleting an unsourced number is correct, not a loss — every numeric field is reviewed automatically, so a number the book does not state cannot survive review in any form. This never applies to a printed value you can see on the page: find its page rather than deleting it.

Apply findings within the original purpose/focus/question. A previous review cannot enlarge that scope merely by naming more facts on viewed pages. Distinguish a sourced conflict or necessary current dependency from an unrelated suggestion; do not make every suggested neighbor ready to silence it. Preserve supported facts and their evidence, and explain why any disputed omission is outside the requested use when submitting the same scoped material for fresh review.

Write draft.json over as many write/edit calls as needed. The visual shard has these fields:

- `interaction_scene`: required only for an opening with task.opening_scope first_interaction; the semantic node id of its first substantive playable interaction. Independent review checks the selection and the source-supported path from the entry.
- `source_needs`: retained questions as {kind,focus,question,reason,trigger,source_refs}. Kind is source_read, runtime_context, deferred or uncertain. Write the question/reason/trigger in English, and name a person there exactly as the book prints the name, never a translation or a transliteration of it. A current missing authored fact stays source_read (unclear classification stays uncertain); submit_reading will retain it and request another native source lookup. A missing live input or discretionary choice is runtime_context with the relevant sourced condition retained in the graph. A later optional source use is deferred, with an exact thin/accepted entity focus, original-page references and its future trigger. These entries survive into source lookup and background work. Never empty the array just to pass the checker. The reviewer receives the original retained requests even after an author removes them from the candidate.

- `nodes`: existing node vocabulary; each has `node_id`, `node_kind`, `name`, `source_refs`, optional `aliases`, `summary`, `properties`, `visibility`, and `distinct_from` only as described under task.roster below. An id is kind + ASCII kebab name. Source references are `[{"page":5}]`, optionally with `box`. Only use pages actually viewed.
- `claims`: `subject_id`, `predicate`, `object:{node_id}`, `truth_status`, `source_refs`, optional `reason`, `visibility`, `known_by_ids`, `asserted_by_ids`, `validity`. Claim identifiers are optional and generated by the host. known_claims provides already accepted statements: preserve their wording and conditions, or omit unchanged optional fields when adding corroborating source references. Do not rephrase an existing reason. Distinct assertions about the same endpoints need distinct semantic claim identifiers. Do not write a second relations array.
- `node_refs`: identifiers of existing nodes referenced but not redeclared. Reuse the known roster and aliases. Add only newly sourced properties to an existing node. known_nodes.ready is host metadata: do not copy it into a draft node. Preserve ready nodes' accepted summaries and values. A not-ready navigation node may receive a complete source-grounded summary when this task first prepares it; its identity and prior structured facts still stay intact.
- task.cast_names, when present, lists every individual the whole book names: `book` holds the forms the book prints, `play` their play-language renderings. A person you declare who is listed there takes one of the printed forms as `name`, exactly as listed, and the other printed forms as `aliases`, so the same individual keeps one identity across readings.
- task.roster, when present, comes first in your task: every published node that cites one of this job's pages, as `{id, kind, name, aliases, pages}`. A thing listed there, or anywhere in known_nodes, is already in the graph: write its new facts under that `id` and never declare a second node for it. The check refuses a new node of the same kind as a published node when the new node's name is one of the published node's names or the published node's name is one of the new node's, and a new person the book's cast holds as the same individual as a published one (`duplicate_of_published`); each finding names the published node with its names, pages and summary. Answer it by deleting your node and using the published id, pointing your claims, node_refs, ready_nodes, critical pointers and source needs at it. Only when your pages name a different thing that shares the name, keep your node and add `"distinct_from": ["<published id>"]` to it; the independent reviewer checks that against the page, and an unsupported distinct_from refuses the reading.
- `coverage`: an object that maps each domain this task actually prepared (from `task.vocabulary.coverage_domains`) to one status (from `task.vocabulary.coverage_status`), for example `{"actors": "accepted", "knowledge": "partial"}`; `{}` when no domain is prepared. It is never an array and never names pages.
- `dependencies`: unresolved source dependencies of the current prepared scope, as `{focus, question, pages?}`. Resolve them by reading; publication requires an empty array. Do not hide unresolved dependencies to pass.
- `critical`: JSON pointers to important source claims within the draft, including conditions, identity, lies, and causal or mechanical statements. Every numeric properties field, every field under a node's `mechanics` and every claim also requires review automatically.
- `ready_nodes`: ids whose material is actually prepared by this task. Each must appear in nodes so the fresh review can check it; for an already-ready node include only its identity and newly sourced fields needed for this question. The host preserves its accepted fields; do not copy its entire dossier into the delta. A thin, known destination is not ready merely because it is named. Include the opening scene and the people/clues/rules it needs; leave genuinely unprepared neighbors out.

You are extracting facts, not inventing a scenario. Preserve motive, fear, secret, voice, knowledge, belief, lie and relationships when the book provides them. Put factual prose in NPC properties (knowledge, beliefs, lies, biography); a person's `summary` says who they are only as the page says it (alive or dead, whose kin, rank, office or trade): attach each modifier to the noun the page attaches it to, because the independent reviewer checks every such statement against the page and refuses one the page does not make of this person. Give a person `properties.appearance` when the book describes how they look, dress or behave when the investigators first meet them (a "how to play" line counts): the book's words, nothing they hide, nothing that infects or controls them, no crime, past or later reveal; leave it out when the book describes none. claims are meaningful relations between nodes, not containers for arbitrary sentences. A belief about a person must not point to that person's true secret as if the believer knows it. Keeper instructions belong in scene keeper_notes or NPC keeper_note, never in an NPC agenda or secret. Preserve numerical values, table rows and their conditions, and distinguish optional rules or variant openings. A sidebar is not automatically a scene. A map or handout can use the existing asset/handout kinds and a source region; do not invent node kinds for visual layouts. Keep Keeper-only annotations private. Do not put preparation status, TODOs or reader instructions into authored summaries or properties.

Preserve operational constraints as carefully as numbers: explicit no-roll actions, which exact obstacle requires a check, conditional hazards, and route junctions. Include these constraints in critical and in source-backed rule nodes linked with uses-rule when appropriate. Do not move a chimney's climbing rule onto a neighboring pit or turn a side chamber into the only passage. For spatial scenes read both the map and surrounding prose; preserve connected sublocations and branches using the existing vocabulary rather than leaving connectivity inside a truncated overview.

When a page states that a place demands a meeting or a check before the investigators get something there (access, a way onward, a person's help), also write that demand as a `requirement` node beside the rule node that keeps its wording. Its `properties.obligation` is `{"scene", "trigger", "who"?, "reaction"?, "demand", "settles"}`: `scene` is the scene node where the demand stands; `trigger` is `{"kind":"attempt","guards":{"clues"?,"exits"?,"people"?}}` naming the clue nodes, destination scene nodes or npc nodes it withholds, or `{"kind":"after","obligation":<requirement node_id>}` when it follows another stated demand; `who` is the person who imposes it; `demand` lists the stated steps in order, each `{"kind":"meet","npc"}`, `{"kind":"cost","book"}` or `{"kind":"check","scope":"actor-target","target"}` (`"scope":"actor"` without a target when nobody opposes it) with `"values":[{"path":"skills.<name>" or "characteristics.<name>","label","minimum"?}]`, `"selection":"approach"` (the investigator uses one of them) or `"maximum"` (the higher one), `"difficulty"` (`regular`, `hard` or `extreme`), `"results"` giving each of `critical`, `extreme`, `hard`, `regular`, `failure` and `fumble` as `{"settles":true|false,"book"?}`, and optional `"push":{"allowed","book"?}`; `settles` is `{"kind":"flag_set","flag_id":<a new kebab id>}`. A `book` line is the page's consequence in one English line for the Keeper; a `minimum` is a rating the page states as a threshold. Name each skill and characteristic as the rules name them, not as the page translates them; the checker lists the rules' names when one does not resolve. When the page states no difficulty, write `"difficulty_unstated":true` and no difficulty; when it names no skill, write `"approaches_unstated":true` and no values or selection; never fill either from habit. Write `"reaction":"preordained"` only when the page states that the person's reaction roll is not used. The person must be present-in that scene on the page's own evidence. Link the node with a `has-requirement` claim from the scene and a `calls-for-check` claim from the requirement to the rule node, and list every obligation field in critical. A hazard that strikes when nobody sought anything, a reward, and what usually happens are not demands; a page that states none gets none.

When a page states a rule the rules engine can use — a roll and its difficulty, damage, a Sanity loss, a time or resource cost, a hazard, an actor's numbers, weapons and defence, a spell's or a tome's costs, what an ending grants — write it as a typed shape in `properties.mechanics` of the node that states it, beside the book's wording in `summary`. Do not invent a key of your own for a mechanic (a damage, loss or skill sentence under a name you chose): the rules engine reads only these shapes. `mechanics` holds only these keys, each on its own node kinds: `profile` (npc, creature); `check`, `hazard`, `damage`, `time_cost` (rule, hazard); `sanity_loss`, `resource_cost` (rule, hazard, object, artifact); `weapon` (object, artifact); `spell` (spell); `tome` (tome); `reward` (rule). A node carries at most one of each; two separately stated rolls are two rule nodes. Link a rule or hazard node from its scene with `uses-rule`, as before. Two laws hold for every shape. Accounting: when the page states the mechanic but leaves a needed value out, write `<slot>_unstated: true` in place of that value; never write both, and never fill a value from habit, the core rules or what usually happens. A page that states no mechanic gets no shape, and a shape is never written to make a node look complete. Dice: a dice slot holds only the bare expression in capitals, such as `1D6`, `1D4+2` or `2D6+1D4`; never words or units (`1D4+2 hit points` is refused: the unit is the shape and the words go in its `book`), never the damage bonus (`1D6+DB` is refused: a weapon that adds it says `"adds_damage_bonus":true`). A fixed quantity is an integer. Any shape may carry one `book`: the page's remaining words in one English line for the Keeper. Name each skill and characteristic as the rules name them, for example `Fighting (Brawl)` or `Language (Other: <the language>)`, not as the page translates them; the checker lists the rules' names when one does not resolve. Legacy tasks require review of every mechanics field. Under module-logic-v1, review the record and its causal/applicability relationships; ordinary parameter differences stay advisory.

`check`, a stated roll: `{"scope":"actor"`, or `"actor-target"` with `"target":<npc or creature id>`, or `"opposed"` with `"opposing":{"values":[...],"selection":"maximum"}`; `"values":[{"path":"skills.<name>" or "characteristics.<name>","label","minimum"?}]`; `"selection":"maximum"` (one roll against the higher, as in "X or Y") or `"approach"` (the investigator uses one of them); `"difficulty":"regular"|"hard"|"extreme"`; optional `"results"` naming only the levels the page states among `critical`, `extreme`, `hard`, `regular`, `failure`, `fumble`, each `{"effects"?,"book"?}`; optional `"push":{"allowed","effects"?,"book"?}}`. When the page names no difficulty write `"difficulty_unstated":true`; when it names no skill write `"approaches_unstated":true` and no values or selection. An effect is one of `{"kind":"damage","dice"}`, `{"kind":"sanity_loss","success","failure"}`, `{"kind":"time","amount","unit"}`, `{"kind":"cost","resource","amount"}`, `{"kind":"flag","flag_id":<a new kebab id>,"value":true}`, `{"kind":"clock","clock_id","ticks"}`, `{"kind":"book","line"}`, and inside a hazard `{"kind":"next_step","step"}`. Example, a page saying that climbing the rope needs a Hard Climb roll and that a failure means a fall for 1D6 damage: `"check":{"scope":"actor","values":[{"path":"skills.Climb","label":"Climb"}],"selection":"maximum","difficulty":"hard","results":{"failure":{"effects":[{"kind":"damage","dice":"1D6"}]}}}`.

`hazard`, a danger the page states and the Keeper triggers: `{"trigger":{"kind":"keeper"}` (the Keeper decides when), `{"kind":"enter"}` (stated on entering) or `{"kind":"attempt","guards":{"clues"?,"exits"?,"people"?}}` (stated when they try to reach those clue, scene or person ids); optional `"when"`, a condition written `{"kind":"always"}`, `{"kind":"clue_discovered","clue_id"}` or `{"kind":"flag_set","flag_id","value"?}`; optional `"steps"`, the checks in the page's order, where a step's result names the next with `{"kind":"next_step","step":<its index>}`; optional `"effects"` for what happens with no roll`}`. Example, a page saying the rotten floor gives way under anyone who fails a Luck roll, and that a Jump roll then saves them from a 1D6 fall, with no difficulty printed for either roll: `"hazard":{"trigger":{"kind":"keeper"},"steps":[{"scope":"actor","values":[{"path":"characteristics.Luck","label":"Luck"}],"selection":"maximum","difficulty_unstated":true,"results":{"failure":{"effects":[{"kind":"next_step","step":1}]}}},{"scope":"actor","values":[{"path":"skills.Jump","label":"Jump"}],"selection":"maximum","difficulty_unstated":true,"results":{"failure":{"effects":[{"kind":"damage","dice":"1D6"}]}}}]}`. An explicit no-roll permission is not a hazard; keep it where the clue and rule instructions above put it.

`damage`, hit points lost to something other than an attack (a fall, glass, fire): `{"dice":"1D4+2"}`, or `{"dice_unstated":true}` when the page says harm is taken but prints no amount. `sanity_loss`, Sanity lost on success and on failure: a printed `1/1D6` is `{"success":"1","failure":"1D6"}`, each half a string (`"0"`, a number or dice); `success_unstated` or `failure_unstated` for a side the page does not print. It sits on the rule or hazard for what is witnessed, on the object or artifact for seeing the thing; an actor's own goes in its profile. `time_cost`: `{"amount":3,"unit":"hour"}`, the amount an integer or dice such as `"1D10"`, with `unit` one of `round`, `minute`, `hour`, `day`, `week`, `"minimum":true` for "at least"; a span with no count of a unit ("an afternoon", "each half-day") is `{"amount_unstated":true,"book":"<the span>"}`, never converted. `resource_cost`: `{"resource":"mp"|"pow"|"san"|"luck"|"hp","amount":<integer or dice>,"per"?:"use"|"round"|"cast"}`, with `"chosen":true` instead of an amount when the page lets the spender choose, or `"amount_unstated":true`; money is not a resource cost.

`weapon`, a weapon or attack: `{"name","skill":<skill name>,"damage":<dice>,"uses_per_round":<integer>,"impale":<true or false>,"adds_damage_bonus"?:true,"base_range_yards"?,"magazine"?,"malfunction"?}`, each of `skill`, `damage`, `uses_per_round`, `impale` stated or `_unstated`. It sits in `mechanics.weapon` of an object or artifact, or in an actor's `profile.weapons`. Example, a creature printed with "Claw 45%, damage 1D6 + damage bonus": `{"name":"Claw","skill":"Fighting (Brawl)","damage":"1D6","adds_damage_bonus":true,"uses_per_round_unstated":true,"impale_unstated":true}` in `profile.weapons`, with `"Fighting (Brawl)":45` in `profile.skills`. When the page states how an npc or creature fights, write `properties.combat` (outside `mechanics`) with only what the page states: `"defense":"dodge"|"fight_back"|"none"` for how it defends, `"action":"attack"` only when the page says it always attacks, and `"disposition":"fights_to_the_end"|"fights_then_flees"|"avoids_fighting"|"surrenders"` for how it behaves in a fight.

`spell`, on the spell node: `{"cost_mp":<integer or dice>` (or `"cost_mp_chosen":true` when the caster chooses, or `cost_mp_unstated`), `"cost_sanity":<integer or dice>` (or `cost_sanity_unstated`), optional `"cost_pow"`, `"casting_time":<a time_cost>`, optional `"duration":<a time_cost>`, optional `"effects":[{"kind":"hp"|"san"|"mp","amount","direction":"gain"|"loss"}]}`. Example, "Cost: 1D6 magic points; 1 Sanity point; casting time: 1 round": `{"cost_mp":"1D6","cost_sanity":1,"casting_time":{"amount":1,"unit":"round"}}`. `tome`, on the tome node: `{"initial_reading":<a time_cost>}` plus what the page prints among `"language":"skills.<name>"`, `"read_check":<a check>`, `"read_without_roll_at":<integer>`, `"full_study":<a time_cost>`, `"cthulhu_mythos_initial"`, `"cthulhu_mythos_full"`, `"mythos_rating"`, `"max_sanity_reduction"` (integers), `"sanity_cost":<a sanity_loss>` and `"spells":[<spell node ids>]`. A printed Sanity loss goes in `sanity_cost` as its two halves, a side the page does not print marked unstated. Example, "written in an ancient language; initial reading 2 weeks; Cthulhu Mythos +2/+5": `{"language":"skills.Language (Other) (<that language>)","initial_reading":{"amount":2,"unit":"week"},"cthulhu_mythos_initial":2,"cthulhu_mythos_full":5}`. `reward`, on a rule node linked by `uses-rule` from the ending or conclusion scene: `{"sanity":"1D6","cash"?:<number>,"currency"?:<one unit label>,"when"?:<a condition>}`, or `"sanity_unstated":true` when the page grants Sanity without an amount.

Investigation material has two parts when the source supports both: a physical carrier and the proposition an investigator can learn. A carrier is an artifact, object, document or handout; a clue is the discoverable proposition, written in the clue summary, that matters to the prepared investigation. For example, "a written page exists" is carrier material, while "that page states proposition X" is a clue proposition. Keep the carrier and create the sourced clue; do not use a carrier at a scene as a substitute for the clue chain. Delivery contract: put source-backed metadata on clue properties using existing `delivery_kind`, `delivery`, `skill` and `difficulty`. Use `delivery_kind: "skill_check"` for a required skill check, with named `skill`/`difficulty` when sourced; `npc_dialogue` for source-authored NPC disclosure, with timing or conditions in `delivery` and the source NPC connected by `knows`; and `obvious` for authored automatic observation. Other modes should keep source prose in `delivery`; `environmental` and `handout` remain possible descriptions, but unknown metadata is not a guaranteed disclosure or no-roll claim. Omit absent values and unclear kinds; do not write `skill: null`, aliases such as `skill`/`conversation`, reinterpret legacy `conversation` as automatic, or make a guaranteed handoff merely because an NPC knows the clue. Preserve explicit no-roll and conditional rules in rule nodes linked with `uses-rule`. Place the clue in its actual discoverable scene with `discoverable-at`, connect it to source-supported conclusions with `supports`, and connect a knowing NPC with `knows` when appropriate. Do not infer clues from keyword tables, invent conclusions or support edges, or create material merely to satisfy a gate. Coverage review checks that supported delivery semantics reach these runtime fields/tags rather than synonym keys; it has no count gate. Some prepared scopes legitimately contain no clues or conclusions.

Runtime facts that must be connected:

- Scenes are places the player can occupy. Connect authored exits using route-to or other supplied route relations; print order is not causation. A scene declares properties.is_entrance or properties.is_final only when the book supports it.
- An ending node is a possible authored settlement, not a place to walk. A scene-to-ending may-lead-to relation is shown as an advisory close with its narrative conditions; it is not a direct movement exit and does not require every intermediate scene to be ready. Scene-to-scene route relations do represent movement or playable transitions and must be licensed by the source.
- The module node in known_nodes holds book-wide properties such as era and entry_scene_ids/ending_scene_ids. Declare alternatives honestly; do not guess among multiple authored openings. Leave ending_scene_ids absent when endings have not been read; unread does not mean the book has no ending. When the book states when its story opens, record that moment on the module node as well: `start_clock.local_datetime` (local date and time, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM`) when both a date and a clock time are authored, or `start_time` (`HH:MM`) when only a clock hour is authored. Cite the stating page like any other fact and list the field in critical. Never guess, deduce from real-world history, or approximate an opening moment the pages do not state; a book that says neither carries none.
- Place actors with present-in, clues with discoverable-at, and connect clues to conclusions with supports.
- A scene the book paces carries the book's pacing on the scene node: `dramatic_question` (the question the scene turns on, when the book states or plainly implies one), `pressure_moves` (the moves the book gives the Keeper when the party stalls there), `exit_conditions` (what the book says ends the scene) and `keeper_notes` (the book's advice to the Keeper for that scene, never its secrets). Write only what the pages support; a scene the book does not pace carries none of them. When the book gives a scenario-wide tension curve or horror escalation, express it as one `beat` node per paced scene with `scene_id`, `tension_target` (low, medium, high, climax), `horror_stage` (ordinary, wrongness, pattern, revelation) and a one-line `note`; a `beat` is the book's pacing, not a place. Place locations with occurs-at / located-in. A scene's authored mechanical rules connect through uses-rule. Never create a clue solely to satisfy a gate.

present-in means physically at hand in that scene. A person mentioned in a letter, will, rumor or history is not thereby standing there. A dead or absent person can remain unplaced; do not put them on stage to satisfy a structural check. Later actors outside the current opening do not need to be prepared now.

For an already-ready entity, write a small additive delta, not a replacement dossier. Omit unchanged summaries, properties and claims. Review checks the new facts against their new citations; do not force old facts onto the current pages. Existing accepted context remains available in task.known_nodes and task.known_claims. For a person's dossier, use the exact property keys and claim predicates in task.vocabulary.actor_dossier. Those names reach the existing NPC projection; do not hide the same material under newly invented parallel labels.
- Use the existing properties for source facts. Scalar facts belong in properties, while claims connect nodes. A character's stats are a structured block, not a compressed prose summary.

Persons and creatures: decide each being's kind per node and per encounter from how the book treats it, never from its species. An `npc` is someone the book lets the investigators deal with as a person (talk to, bargain with, persuade, argue down, call by name), with a name to learn and motives of its own; a monster, a ghost or a captive alien that answers questions is an npc, and so is a person who does not speak. A `creature` is a being the book presents only as a body: an animal, a swarm, a mindless monster, or a mind that never deals with anyone. One species can be both in one book: the leader who bargains is an npc, the feral pack of its kind a creature. A life stage the book gives its own numbers is its own node; a parasite with its own body and numbers is a creature beside its host; a being with a human and a monstrous form is one npc. One being is one node: never draft an npc and a creature with the same name or handle; the checker refuses the pair. A creature's numbers and fighting go in `properties.mechanics.profile` and `properties.combat` as for any actor, but it gets no person dossier: write only the creature words in task.vocabulary.creature_dossier (its profile_keys and each contributed entry's ask), under those exact keys, when the book states them.

Source weaknesses are base book facts, independent of consumer Mod switches. For every npc and creature read in this task, review the original pages for weaknesses, resistance and immunity. Use task.vocabulary.actor_weaknesses and write what the book says harms, repels, binds, banishes or ends an npc or creature as `properties.weaknesses`, a list of `{"book", "needs"?, "learned_by"?}`. `book` (required) is one English line in the book's terms with its conditions and degree; a stat-line resistance or immunity ("only fire, magic and electricity harm it; firearms do nothing") is a `book` line here, never a keeper_note. `needs` lists the node_ids of the means the book names, of the kinds in task.vocabulary.actor_weaknesses.node_refs.needs.kinds: an object, artifact, spell, tome or place, or the person who must be there or whose end ends the being; one means may serve many beings. `learned_by` is the node_id of the `conclusion` the investigators can reach that states the weakness, supported by the clues that teach it (each clue `supports` it and is placed where it is found, as for any clue). Leave `learned_by` out when the book gives no route: such a weakness is found in play. What the book does not give is absent, never invented: no entry, means or route to make a being look beatable. List each entry's pointer in critical.

A clue whose belief about a being is false (folklore that silver stops it, a rite that does nothing) links to that npc or creature with `misleads`, which runs only from a clue to an npc or creature. A false lead against a weakness the book also states truly is `contradicts` from the clue to that weakness's conclusion.

For a player-facing handout or map, declare `properties.image_sources` as a list of `{page, box?}` regions that are safe to reveal.
