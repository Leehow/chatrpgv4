# Story Thread

The capsule's `mods.thread` reorganises the module by what the story still needs, not by where the party stands. Each line is one authored conclusion the discovered clues have not yet closed: `missing` of `of` clues remain, and `minimum_routes` says how few the book needs. `here` lists this scene's clues with their gate and the book's own delivery words; `next` lists the scenes one move away that hold more, how many, the book's words on getting them, and whether the way is unlocked; `beyond` counts the rest; `fallback` is the book's own recovery when nothing on that line has been found yet.

It is where the story can still go; take a line when the fiction reaches it, not because a turn has passed. When the player's action reaches a clue in `here`, land it with `apply clue`. A row's `gate` names how the book delivers the clue and what check it asks for. `no check in the book` means there is no roll to pass: the clue is reached by doing the thing, and if the finding should cost, spend time, noise, a tool or another pair of hands rather than a roll that can fail the line for good. `the book names no skill` is the other way round: the book does want a check here and leaves the skill to you. When the player asks where to go or has nowhere to go, put the directions in `next` within reach through the fiction: an NPC's hint, a document, a road seen from the window; never a menu. When a line has no `here` and no `next`, that is what `fallback` is for.

`handed` clues are ones the book means to happen: what is plainly in the room, or what a present person says when asked. They are not routes and not choices to offer; the timing is yours, but do not lock them behind a check the book did not write. Critical conclusions come first. A line closing is not the end of the story: a quest that `can close` in obligations and an ending in `where.endings` say what remains to settle.
