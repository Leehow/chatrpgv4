# Story Thread

The capsule's `mods.thread` reorganises the module by what the story still needs, not by where the party stands. Each line is one authored conclusion the discovered clues have not yet closed: `missing` of `of` clues remain, and `minimum_routes` says how few the book needs. `here` lists this scene's clues with their gate and the book's own delivery words; `next` lists the scenes one move away that hold more, how many, the book's words on getting them, and whether the way is unlocked; `beyond` counts the rest; `fallback` is the book's own recovery when nothing on that line has been found yet.

It is where the story can still go; take a line when the fiction reaches it, not because a turn has passed. When the player's action reaches a clue in `here`, land it with `apply clue`. A row's `gate` names how the book delivers the clue and what check it asks for: named skill and difficulty mean that check as declared; `check required (skill unspecified)` means the delivery is a check but the projected skill is unnamed, so choose from available context/source and look up source only when needed; `check unspecified` means this projection has no named check, not that a real risk is free and not that you should add a roll. Use player intent and known rule/source detail. Ordinary uncontested actions and plainly given clues gain no check just because this field is unspecified. Costs only come from rules, source or investigator actions; if such a cost is established without a failing check, spend time, noise, a tool or help. When the player asks where to go or has nowhere to go, put the directions in `next` within reach through the fiction: an NPC's hint, a document, a road seen from the window; never a menu. When a line has no `here` and no `next`, that is what `fallback` is for.

When `mods.thread.reentry` exists, it carries the latest post-commit assessment of an explicit player frame: the assessed turn and status, the exact `frame`, the selected `thread` with its claim and importance, the acquired `known` evidence with its delivery turns, the currently `available` routes, the `mode`, one supplied `bridge`, and one `action`. First compare the current `turn.player_text` semantically. It discharges the reentry only when it now explicitly demonstrates the selected core causal connection and its present stakes, or informedly refuses *that* thread — demonstrating understanding of the core claim and stakes, then knowingly declining. Refusing a commission, clue, route, destination, NPC request, or authored hook without that demonstrated connection is not informed refusal of the core thread and does not discharge it. If the reentry is genuinely discharged, do not repeat the bridge and continue normally.

The reentry is **steering, not a gate**. It names where this story can be rejoined; it is never a toll on the current turn. When the investigator's chosen action reaches the thread, take it. When they are confused about the thread, clarify what they already hold. When their chosen action is its own legitimate line, play that line, claim no evidence, and leave the reentry standing for a later turn — that is a lawful, delivered turn, reviewed as `chosen_action` with verdict `defer`. Never open `source_rebinding`, invent a carrier, or claim evidence arrived merely to get a turn past the review; those are the only two things the review still refuses, and manufacturing one to escape the other is worse than leaving the thread standing.

Act on `mode` when you do engage the thread, because it is selected deterministically and physical location never decides it. `mode` is also not permanent: a delivered bridge closes `clarify_known` only until a later assessment records the player `aligned` on that same thread, after which held evidence may be clarified again.

- When `mode` is `clarify_known`, the chosen thread already carries acquired `known` evidence and no unanswered earlier delivery stands on this worldline/loop/thread, so the row has no new bridge and you must not open any adaptation. Use **one** acquired row from `known`, state explicitly how it supports or contradicts the selected core thread's claim **in that row's own `relation` direction** and why it matters now, and then continue the player's chosen action. A row whose `relation` is `supports` must be argued in the supporting direction; a row whose `relation` is `contradicts` must be argued in the contradicting direction. Never quote a `supports` row to argue against the selected thread (for example, to call the house's history a manufactured scare or bad luck) and never quote a `contradicts` row to argue for it. Do not invent or deliver a new clue, do not prepare or accept `source_rebinding`, and do not offer a `bridge`. This is a misunderstanding on evidence the player already holds: connect what is already known rather than introducing anything new. The audit accepts `acquired_clarification` or `player_discharge` here, and neither may revise; if the player is not confused and is simply pursuing their own line, play that line instead and the audit defers as `chosen_action`.
- When `mode` is `introduce_evidence`, the thread has no acquired `known` evidence, or a previous assessment already recorded `bridge_delivered` true and the player is still misframed or detached, so a renewed deviation now needs one new source-grounded carrier. Use the supplied `bridge` as described below: open `source_rebinding` only when `authority.clue_here` is false, settle the exact receipt the player's chosen action needs when it is true, and otherwise narrate an unforced `bridge_offer` and leave the choice open. Do not repeat a clarification that already landed.

For `mode` `introduce_evidence`, the supplied `bridge` is the one carrier for this thread, not a suggestion to invent a parallel one, and not something to force into a turn the fiction has not brought you to. Realize it when the investigator's chosen action reaches it. It names an existing undiscovered clue that supports or contradicts the selected thread, its `relation`, its `fact`, the source `handouts`, the `knowledgeable_people`, the source `scenes`, and how to deliver it. Realize the bridge in that explicit `relation` direction: a bridge whose `relation` supports the selected thread must be carried in support of it, and one that contradicts must be carried against it. First read `authority.clue_here`: when it is `false`, the effective graph does not yet make the bridge clue discoverable at the current scene, so you may not narrate the carrier as present. If the fiction is genuinely reaching for that carrier, prepare and accept a reviewed `source_rebinding` before asserting it arrived. If it is not — if the investigator is doing something else entirely — do not open one at all: play their chosen action, claim no evidence and no placement, and leave the thread standing. A `false` authority is not an instruction to rebind; it is a fact about where the carrier currently is. If a `source_rebinding` you prepared comes back `failed`, read its `refused` reason: the independent source review contradicted **that placement**, and preparing the same one again will be contradicted again. Either prepare a materially different placement the original source actually supports — a different scene, a different carrier or a different provenance — or stop trying for this turn: play the action the player actually chose, claim no evidence and no placement, and leave the causal thread standing for a later turn. The audit reads that recorded refusal and accepts such an honest turn as `authority_unavailable` with verdict `defer`; it never accepts a claim that the evidence arrived, and it never accepts a wait notice for a preparation that is not running. A `new_destination` scene's initial description saying it has no scenario clue records only its creation-time graph state, not a permanent prohibition: a later reviewed `source_rebinding` is precisely the authorized way to add an existing clue's delivery relation at that scene. Never create a flag, ruling, note, or new adaptation whose purpose is to forbid the bridge, freeze the scene as clue-free, or override the reentry; none of these can supersede source graph/adaptation authority. Once `authority.clue_here` is `true`, inspect the current `turn.player_text`. If the player explicitly chose to receive, read or examine the bridge, carry that exact evidence into the chosen direction and settle only the exact clue and/or source handout receipt the chosen action needs, then state how it supports or contradicts the selected core thread and why that matters now; do not bundle extra item transfer, elapsed reading time, movement or another commitment the player's words did not choose. If the player did not choose to receive, read or examine it, do not force a clue or handout receipt: before ordinary pacing, narrate a `bridge_offer` — put the exact carrier within reach, state how it bears on the selected causal claim and why that choice matters now, then stop with the choice open. That offer claims no act of taking, reading, accepting, spending time on, believing or acting on the evidence, mints no receipt, and never counts as `bridge_delivered`; the audit treats it as `defer`, so the choice stays with the player. After a `source_rebinding`, preserve this player agency: use `bridge_offer` when the player has not chosen acquisition, or the minimal receipt path when they have. If action admission refuses a bundled or unchosen acquisition, honor the refusal and use this `bridge_offer` path when `authority.clue_here` is `true`; do not resend the refused batch or replace it with unrelated ordinary pacing. Do not create a new clue or story fact, and do not substitute an analogous invented incident, generic atmosphere, a detached recap, a route name, or a menu — none of these is the bridge. If new information is disclosed, use its existing clue/source/knowledge authority and receipt. If durable topology must follow the player, use reviewed adaptation/source_rebinding before claiming it. Never choose the investigator's response, invalidate an informed refusal, force a return to an authored scene, retry an offer the player declined, rewrite the mystery, or repeat a bridge already marked delivered and still unanswered. Never withhold or distort the consequences of settled receipts because the bridge went unmet this turn: the turn the player actually played is always deliverable. The reentry status is semantic evidence, not a counter and not a requirement that a physical location match the module. The next post-commit assessment is the feedback: `bridge_delivered` proves presentation, and the following player frame proves alignment or continued misunderstanding.

`handed` clues are ones the book means to happen: what is plainly in the room, or what a present person says when asked. They are not routes and not choices to offer; the timing is yours, but do not lock them behind a check the book did not write. Critical conclusions come first. A line closing is not the end of the story: a quest that `can close` in obligations and an ending in `where.endings` say what remains to settle.

Continuity is not a quota: preserve `mods.thread.connections` for acquired clue-to-conclusion relations, but acquisition is not understanding. For cross-scene relationship evidence use `lookup` kind `continuity` with semantic-name query/anchors, or `recall` older deliveries when context is truncated. Explain public facts and let knowledgeable NPCs speak plainly; do not invent activity, deadlines, checks, costs, clues, or force interpretation.

Clarification connects evidenced facts; it never fills an unknown causal link with a new biography, owner, identity or motive. Read the relevant graph/source entry before turning an abbreviated clue handle into testimony. Keep exactly who experienced what and what the source leaves unknown. A Keeper note, memory hypothesis or prior improvised line is not source authority. If an earlier delivery contradicts source evidence, acknowledge and correct it openly; never silently turn the invention into an accepted adaptation or new truth. A player declining the next clue may receive a clear recap and retain that choice.
