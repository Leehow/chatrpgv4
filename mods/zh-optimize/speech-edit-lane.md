The table's owner finds such lines read like written text, not like people talking: they lack the spoken connectives and modal particles that join sentences and carry tone.

Then edit it the way the demonstrations do, choosing only words whose function fits that act and the speaker's mood:

Connectives and discourse markers, by function:
- taking the turn or moving on: 好、那、那么、行
- the next step in a sequence: 然后、再
- spelling out what was just said: 就是、就是说、我的意思是
- starting an explanation: 是这样的、你听我说
- turning or conceding: 不过、但是、可、倒是
- moving to the other part of a question: 至于 (rarely)
- adding a separate item: 另外、还有、对了 (a thing just remembered)
- whatever the case: 反正
- correcting an impression or admitting frankly: 其实、说实话、说白了
- offering, toward the listener: 您看、你看、要不……吧 (only when the speaker is genuinely offering or suggesting)
- seeking agreement: 对吧、你知道吧、是不是
Modal particles, by tone: 吧 (suggesting, unsure, softening an order) · 呢 (an ongoing state, asking back, contrast) · 啊 (softening, exclaiming, urging, impatience) · 嘛 (it is obvious; a mildly impatient explanation) · 了 (a change, or the end of it) · 呀、啦、哦、咯 (lighter variants).

Rules:
- Keep the facts, the speaker's stance and mood, and most of the wording; these are small edits, like the demonstrations.
- Add a word only where a person would actually say it; not every sentence needs one. Use each connective at most once in this turn, and do not start several lines with the same one.
- Never turn a statement or an instruction into a question unless the speaker is offering something.
- Replace clever quips, parallel retorts and point-by-point acknowledgements of what the other person said with plain words or one rhetorical question.
- Prefer spoken words to written ones.
Add no fact and drop no fact; change no number, name, place, condition, refusal or stance. The demonstrations come from another story: never reuse their sentences or their facts. Keep the quotation marks each line uses.
