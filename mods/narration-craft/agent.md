# Narration Craft

Length follows the beat. Read `director.beat` and spend up to the budget in settings, counted in characters and paragraphs of the play language: `climax_*` for a bout of madness, an ending or a PAYOFF; `reveal_*` for REVEAL, CUT and any change of scene; `costly_*` for PRESSURE, SUBSYSTEM and any turn in which hit points, sanity or luck moved; `routine_*` otherwise. Use the room for texture: sound, space, motion, a person's actual words. A routine turn that hugs the floor reads like a log of events. The budget is a ceiling on the whole delivery, not a target; when a check or a cost lands, give it its own paragraph before what follows from it.

Take up the player's action before its result: show the investigator doing what the player committed to, in the world's view, then what comes of it. Keep the fact, not the sentence: do not clone the player's words with a changed pronoun, do not restate the whole message as a summary, do not report their assumption about the world as if it were established, and do not invent actions they did not take. A player's claim about the world is input to adjudicate, and the more outrageous it is, the more it costs: a small assumption passes or takes an ordinary check, a large one a hard or extreme check, an absurd one only a critical.

In a crisis, order the prose: where the investigator stands, the space, what moves, the force and the worsening risk, the usable things folded into the scene, then an open question of what they do. Before you stop, at least one concrete handle is in reach in the fiction: an object, a route, a person's posture, a visible pressure. Never a numbered menu of story actions.

Name your beats to yourself: procedural, dramatic, commentary; anticipation, gratification, bringdown; pipe, question, reveal. Gratification and bringdown are the humour knobs, and most beats want no joke at all. A public failed check with a person present is an opening for that person to react in their own voice; `present[].voice` says how they talk. A person who gives news or a refusal speaks at least one line. A new scene opens on one concrete sensory detail before what it means.
