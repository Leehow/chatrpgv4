## Why this spec exists (recorded verbatim)

The owner, 2026-09-23: "其实所有规则相关的都应该数值化，这样就比较容易用 jev 处理了，pdf 解析也应该有数值化方案". The ruling it produced (`pi-native-single-loop.md`, Rulings): everything the module states as a rule — a check and its difficulty, a damage or sanity die, a time or resource cost, a gate, a hazard trigger, an NPC's numbers and tactic — is authored and extracted as typed values, never as a prose string in an ad-hoc property, so the clerk (Jev, the kernel) can act on it and the Keeper only decides what is not stated. The PDF reader gets the same closed catalog of mechanical shapes as starters and Mods, with provenance and `*_unstated` markers instead of guesses ("accounting, not content"); every shape has a kernel reader (an authored field without a consumer is a defect, not data); and data never changes authority: a stated hazard is still the Keeper's to trigger.

## Problem Statement

The single loop can only hand the clerk what the kernel issues. SL-02's replays showed where it stops: the declared bookkeeping settles before the Keeper is called, and every step that needs a number the book printed goes back to the Keeper, because the number is not data anywhere the kernel reads. The obligation spec found the first instance (the morgue gate was typed in the starter and read by nobody); SL-07 found the second (an NPC's dodge-or-fight-back is stated by nothing). This spec inventories the rest, and the inventory says the problem is general:

- **The PDF-built module carries its mechanics as prose in ad-hoc keys.** Of the 58 property values on the 14 `rule` nodes of `content/starters/the-haunting-rulebook/module-graph.json`, 2 are numbers (`rule-corbitt-combat-order.corbitt_DEX: 35`, `rule-police-file-access.credit_rating_threshold: 75`); the other 56 are sentences (`rule-basement-knife-search.pushed_fail_damage: "1D4+2 hit points"`, `rule-bed-attack.sanity_loss: "SAN 1/1D4 to witness bed move of its own accord"`, `rule-globe-access.skills: "Charm (friendliness), Intimidate (aggression), …"`). The same statement appears under a different key on every node that makes it: sanity loss as `sanity_loss`, `sanity_loss_to_see_attack`, `maximum_sanity_reduction`; damage as `damage`, `fall_damage`, `pushed_fail_damage`, `damage_on_pushed_fail`, `damage_regular`, `damage_table`; a magic-point cost as `magic_point_cost` (string), `magic_point_cost_per_round` (number) and `cost` (string). None of these properties has a typed reader. A rule reaches the Keeper as one line — its `summary`, truncated to 160 characters, at most six per scene (`ModuleGraph.sceneRules`, `kernel-ts/read/module-graph.ts:554-556`, via `listedNodes` `:534-549`; `kernel-ts/read/capsule.ts:202-217`) — and no property of it is projected.
- **The reader is never told the shapes.** `content/setup/visual-reader.md` tells the reader to put explicit checks, hazards and no-roll rules "in source-backed rule nodes linked with uses-rule" (lines 81, 83, 91) and to keep "damage, conditions and other prose in their own fields" (line 104). The only typed mechanical forms it defines are the NPC's `mechanics.profile` (lines 102-104) and a clue's `delivery_kind`/`skill`/`difficulty` (line 83). So the reader invents a key per statement, and the checker accepts any properties (`kernel-ts/modules/visual.ts` `checkDraft`, lines 106-257, checks node fields, maps, NPC numbers at 157-158, visibility and sources — nothing else under `properties`).
- **Dice strings escape independent review.** `numericPaths` (`visual.ts:38-46`) adds a review pointer only for numbers (`numeric`, `kernel-ts/read/values.ts:14`); `"1D4+2 hit points"` is a string, so it reaches `required_review` only if the reader happened to list it in `critical`.
- **The curated starter's typed mechanics sit in places a PDF book can never produce, and several have no reader.** `on_enter.san_triggers`, `on_enter.danger_attacks`, `on_enter.clock_ticks` on scene records, the chapel's `optional_rules`, affordance `time_profile` and `skill_minimums` have zero consumers (D2). A module-specific rules table inside the ruleset, `content/rulesets/coc7/rules-json/the-haunting.json`, carries eight "rules" (Flesh Ward armour dice, the floating knife's MP, the bed attack's `1D6+2`, the basement search's `1D4+2`, the conclusion's `1D6` reward…), of which only its `weapons` list is read (`moduleWeapons`, `kernel-ts/combat/profiles.ts:103-108`, keyed by the module id); the `rules` block also freezes playtest dice (`playtest_roll: 4`, `playtest_die_rolls: [3]`) as if they were rules.
- **The engine reads mechanics in several incompatible ways.** More than ten functions roll or validate dice strings, with different grammars (D4.3). Some read prose on purpose: `parseSanLoss` searches any string for `A/B` (`kernel-ts/sanity/expression.ts:51-55`, unanchored), so Corbitt's `san_loss_to_see: "1/1D8 Sanity points to see him move"` is read by a regex over a sentence; `resolveMpCost` reads the English words `per person` and `variable` (`kernel-ts/magic/engine.ts:44-45`); `weaponDbExpression` reads `half db` inside a weapon's `special` text (`kernel-ts/combat/engine.ts:349-350`). Creature nodes are never read as actors (`npcProfileOf` looks up only `npc` nodes, `kernel-ts/resolve/context.ts:44`), so the twin's `creature-rat-pack` has no stat block anywhere the engine can see.

So, as with obligations, the gap is a **system seam, not missing content**: there is no closed vocabulary for "the book states this number", no validator that holds a reader or an author to it, no kernel reader for most of it, and no way for Jev to bind a parameter the book already fixed. Until that exists, every consequence, cost and hazard sends the loop back to the Keeper to re-derive a number that is printed on the page — and a PDF-built book can never be as playable as a curated starter, because the curated starter's numbers live in side tables a reader cannot write.

## Solution

One **closed catalog of mechanical shapes**, shared by the curated starters, the visual PDF reader and (where a Mod states a mechanic) Mod declarations:

- **One seat.** A node that states a mechanic carries it in `mechanics.<shape>` of its record view (`recordOf(node)`, `module-graph.ts:44-48`: `properties.mechanics` on every node without a starter record, i.e. every PDF-built node and every new node; `runtime_projection.record.mechanics` where a starter node already has a record). The NPC's existing `mechanics.profile` is the first member of the catalog, unchanged. The obligation (`properties.obligation`, §134) and a clue's delivery gate (`clueProfile`) keep their landed seats and are registered as catalog members, not moved.
- **Few shapes, each an existing kernel form.** `check` (the §134.2 declaration form with a third owner), `damage`, `sanity_loss`, `time_cost`, `resource_cost`, `gate`, `hazard`, `obligation`, `stat_block`, `weapon`, `tactic`, `spell`, `tome`, `reward`, `clock` — fifteen names, every one of them built from a form the kernel already reads or validates (D4 names it for each). Numbers are numbers; dice are strings in the one grammar the kernel already validates and rolls; enums are closed; prose is only ever a `book` line for the Keeper.
- **Accounting, not content.** A statement the page makes incompletely is recorded with `<field>_unstated: true`, never a guessed value. The validator refuses malformed shapes (form and references only) and never requires a shape to exist.
- **Every shape has a reader, and the reader decides who may act.** The kernel projects each shape into a seat that already exists (the capsule's `where.rules` rows, the NPC profile the engine builds, `table.apply.options`, the ending lookup, the spell catalog) and binds its values into the operation that uses them. The clerk's authority is unchanged: a stated hazard, a stated sanity loss and a stated cost are still the Keeper's to trigger; what changes is that the Keeper, and Jev when it binds, no longer invent the number.
- **The PDF reader writes the same shapes.** One instruction per shape in `content/setup/visual-reader.md`; the draft check runs the shared validator (as SO-03 does for obligations); every shape field, dice strings included, enters `required_review` and is checked against the page image; existing PDF-built modules gain shapes only through the additive detail path (§22), never by reparsing.

From the player's chair: the numbers at the table are the book's. When the chapel floor gives way, the fall is the book's `1D6`, not a figure the Keeper picked; when the investigator looks at Corbitt, the loss is the book's `1/1D8` whoever routes it; when a PDF book is imported, the same holds for it.

## User Stories

1. As a player, I want the damage, sanity loss and time a stated rule costs me to be the book's numbers, so that the table does not depend on a model's memory of a page.
2. As a player, I want a stated check to be rolled against the skill and difficulty the book states, so that the Keeper's mood does not change how hard the stairs are.
3. As a player, I want a hazard to happen only when the Keeper decides the fiction calls for it, so that data never springs a trap the story did not.
4. As a player, I want the consequence of a failed stated check applied by the Keeper, with the book's amount, so that failure is narrated, not merely computed.
5. As a player, I want a PDF book I import to play with its own printed numbers, so that my book is not a thinner game than the built-in starter.
6. As a player, I want a module that states no mechanic here to play exactly as it does today, so that the change costs nothing where it does not apply.
7. As a Keeper (the model), I want each stated mechanic of the scene in my capsule as one compact typed line beside the rule's wording, so that I know what the book fixes without reading prose properties.
8. As a Keeper, I want to trigger a stated hazard or rule by naming it, so that the kernel binds its skill, difficulty and dice for me.
9. As a Keeper, I want the stated consequence of a result handed back as a bound parameter I may apply, so that I decide whether it happens and never have to recall its dice.
10. As a Keeper, I want my own amount accepted when I improvise past the book, with the receipt saying whose number it was, so that the module stays reference, not scripture.
11. As a Keeper, I want an unstated difficulty or amount marked as unstated, so that I know the book left it to me rather than that the reader forgot it.
12. As a Keeper, I want an NPC's printed numbers, weapons, armour, spells and sanity loss read by the engine whether the book calls it an `npc` or a `creature`, so that I never pin an archetype over a printed stat block.
13. As Jev, I want every parameter of a candidate the book fixes to arrive bound, so that I decide only what is open.
14. As Jev, I want stated consequences, hazards and costs never offered to me as candidates, so that I cannot route a step the rulings keep for the Keeper.
15. As the clerk, I want the NPC's standing defence and the stated obligation check to reach me as data, so that the fight round and the gate close without the LLM (SL-07, SO-04).
16. As the host, I want one validator for every shape, called by starter registration, the reader's draft check and the Mod manifest check, so that no second copy of a shape's rules exists.
17. As the host, I want one dice grammar for authored data, so that a dice string that validates is one every roller accepts.
18. As the host, I want no prose ever parsed into a shape by code, so that no hard-coded reading of English decides a number.
19. As a starter author, I want to declare a mechanic in the module graph with the same keys the reader and a Mod use, so that there is one declaration form to learn.
20. As a starter author, I want the validator to refuse a shape with prose in a typed slot, an unknown skill, a malformed dice string, a dangling reference or a value and its `_unstated` flag both present, so that malformed rules cannot ship.
21. As the PDF reader, I want one instruction per shape, so that I write `mechanics.damage: {dice: "1D4+2"}` instead of inventing `pushed_fail_damage`.
22. As the PDF reader, I want to record what the page leaves out as unstated, so that I never guess a difficulty to make a shape complete.
23. As the independent reviewer, I want every shape field, dice strings included, in my required review, so that I check each against the page image.
24. As a maintainer of an existing PDF-built module, I want shapes added only through the detail path with an explicit question, so that no whole-book reparse runs and earlier generation bytes remain.
25. As a Mod author, I want a mechanic my package states to use the same shape and validator, so that a Mod-declared rule and a book-stated rule are one thing to the kernel.
26. As a maintainer, I want every authored mechanical field in the starters either read by a named kernel reader or deleted, so that no fifth "typed field nobody reads" ships.
27. As a maintainer, I want the curated starter's side table (`the-haunting.json`) reduced to what a graph cannot say, so that a curated starter and a PDF book have the same homes for the same statements.
28. As a maintainer, I want goldens proving that a module without shapes reads byte for byte as before, so that the change is measured where it applies.
29. As the owner, I want each design branch the code could not settle listed as a question with a recommendation, so that I rule once before implementation.

## Implementation Decisions

### D1. What counts as a mechanical statement

A mechanical statement is a sentence of the module (a curated starter, a PDF page, a Mod package) that fixes a value the rules engine consumes: a check (which characteristics or skills, how selected, what difficulty, what each result level does), a die or a quantity (damage, sanity loss or reward, magic points, POW, Luck, money, time, armour, a rating threshold), a condition (what must be true first), an order of such steps, or an actor's numbers, weapons, spells and defence. It is data when all three hold:

1. **Stated.** It is on a page (`source_refs`, and on a starter `evidence_span_ids`) or in the curated starter's authored graph. The reader never derives it from similar books, the rulebook's defaults, or what "usually" happens.
2. **Engine-shaped.** It has one of the catalog's shapes (D4), whose every slot is a number, a dice string in the grammar of D4.3, a closed enum, a reference to a graph node or ruleset table key, or a `book` line.
3. **Owned.** It sits on the node that states it (D3), in exactly one place.

Not mechanical, and never shaped: pressure moves, dramatic questions, "what usually happens", NPC agendas, fears, secrets, voices, tone, consequences phrased only as fiction ("he has them thrown out and barred"; those ride as a `book` line), and any number the page does not print. A statement the page makes but leaves incomplete is shaped with its gap marked (D4.2), not dropped and not filled.

### D3. Principles

- **P1. One seat per statement.** `mechanics.<shape>` of the stating node's record view (`recordOf`), where `<shape>` is a catalog name (D4). A node may carry several shapes (a rule stating a check and a damage), at most one of each name; two statements with a check each are two nodes (a hazard's ordered steps are one statement). The container is closed: an unknown key under `mechanics` is refused. Landed, read seats stay where they are and are registered as the catalog seat for their kind: `mechanics.profile` on actors (the `stat_block`), `properties.obligation` on `requirement` nodes (§134), a clue's canonical `delivery_kind`/`skill`/`difficulty` (the clue gate, `clueProfile`), a route's `when`/`unlock_when` (`gate`), a threat record's `clocks[]` (`clock`), a scene affordance's `rules_operation` of kind `combat_engagement` (§102; starter-only, D10). A second copy of a registered seat is refused where the validator can see both (one check, one owner — the §134.3 `obligation_repeats_clue_gate` rule, generalised).
- **P2. Reuse the kernel's forms.** Every shape is built from the input of an existing verb, decision, engine field or ruleset table (D4 names it). A new form needs a reason, written in D4.
- **P3. One dice grammar for authored data.** D4.3.
- **P4. Accounting, not content.** Each slot that the page may leave out has an `_unstated` twin (D4.2). The validator never requires a shape to exist, never requires a slot to be filled, refuses a value and its `_unstated` flag together, and refuses prose in a typed slot. `document-ir-extraction`'s first rule and the memory ruling "validator checks accounting, not content" apply unchanged.
- **P5. Every shape has a reader before it ships.** A shape is added to the catalog in the same ticket as its kernel reader (D6); a shape with a writer and no reader is the §31 defect and is accepted for one scheduled ticket at most, as §134.7 did. The machine fills what it can derive (a minute count from an hour count, `san_loss` from a `sanity_loss` pair); the model never writes a derivable value.
- **P6. Data never changes authority.** The clerk's list (a)–(d), SO's (e) and `session_step` are unchanged (§135.3 on the SL-02 branch). A shape decides *how much*; who decides *whether* is exactly who decided it before (D8).
- **P7. No prose is ever parsed into a shape by code.** No regex, word list or keyword table reads a sentence into a slot, at build time or at play time. The reader (a model) writes shapes from the page; the reviewer (a model) checks them against the image; the kernel reads only typed slots. Existing prose readers lose their authored inputs as shapes replace them (D9); removing the readers themselves waits until no shipped module feeds them (the control `mystery-house` still does) and is Q5's.
- **P8. The module is reference, not scripture.** The Keeper may always act with its own amount; a stated shape never makes the kernel refuse a Keeper operation (the §134.11 "crossing is information" rule, generalised). Receipts record whose number it was (`basis: stated | keeper`).

### D4. The closed catalog

#### D4.1 The container and where each shape may sit

```
mechanics: {                       // recordOf(node).mechanics; closed keys
  profile?:       stat_block,      // npc, creature              (registered: the existing seat)
  tactic?:        tactic,          // npc, creature              (SL-07)
  check?:         check,           // rule, hazard
  hazard?:        hazard,          // rule, hazard
  damage?:        damage,          // rule, hazard
  sanity_loss?:   sanity_loss,     // rule, hazard, object, artifact (an actor's is profile.sanity_loss)
  time_cost?:     time_cost,       // rule, hazard
  resource_cost?: resource_cost,   // rule, hazard, object, artifact
  weapon?:        weapon,          // object, artifact
  spell?:         spell,           // spell
  tome?:          tome,            // tome
  reward?:        reward           // rule
}
```

Registered seats outside the container (landed and read; the catalog names them, nothing moves): `obligation` at `properties.obligation` of a `requirement` node (§134.1); the clue gate at the clue's canonical `delivery_kind`/`skill`/`difficulty` (§22's clue-profile bridge); `gate` at a route's `when` (and the legacy `unlock_when`/`conditions` the exit reader already accepts, `mg:448-461`); `clock` at a threat record's `clocks[]`.

A shape on a kind outside its row is refused: every shape then has a finite set of readers to test. Each shape may carry one `book` line — the book's words for what the shape cannot type, one English line for the Keeper, exactly as §134.2's `book` (system language, §16). A statement whose entire content is a `book` line is not a shape; it stays in the node's `summary`.

#### D4.2 Accounting, not content

Every slot a page may leave out has a twin `<slot>_unstated: true`. Exactly one of the two is present when the shape's form needs the slot (listed per shape below); neither is present when the slot is optional. The validator refuses both present, and refuses neither present for a needed slot (the author or reader must say which: stated, or not stated). It never refuses a missing shape, never counts shapes, and never checks that a value is plausible. A step with an unstated value is the Keeper's to complete (the Keeper's own value; `basis: keeper`), never the clerk's — the rule §134.2 already fixed for `difficulty_unstated` and `approaches_unstated`, generalised.

#### D4.3 One dice grammar

**Authored dice are validated by one function, `definitionExpression` (`kernel-ts/mods/definition.ts:30-47`):** an integer 0–10000, or `(NdM|N)([+-](NdM|N))*` of at most 80 characters, dice count ≤ 100, faces ≥ 1, every number ≤ 10000; stored uppercased. Every roller that reads authored dice accepts that language: `rollExpression` (`kernel-ts/resolve/arithmetic.ts:30`, behind `apply damage`, sanity, development rewards, armour dice, Mod effects) and `CombatSession.rollDamageExpression` (`kernel-ts/combat/engine.ts:369-393`, case-sensitive, satisfied by the uppercased form). A sanity half is additionally validated by `validateSanLossExpression` (`kernel-ts/sanity/expression.ts:31`: a positive constant or `NdM(+K)`), because that is what the sanity engine rolls. Nothing else is a dice string:

- **No damage bonus inside a string.** `DB`, `½DB`, `+ damage bonus` are refused; a weapon says `adds_damage_bonus: true`, as the weapon-parameter shape already does; an actor's `derived.DB` is one of the ruleset's own damage-bonus values (`content/rulesets/coc7/rules-json/damage-bonus-build.json`), a closed table key, not a free string.
- **No units or words.** `"1D4+2 hit points"`, `"1D6 + 2 (broken glass …)"` are refused; the unit is the shape (damage is hit points), the words are `book`.
- **The engine's other dice readers are not changed by this spec** (the chase's `rollChaseDice`, `rollMagicDice` and `resolveMpCost`, `parseDiceExpression`, `creationRoll`, `reconstructDamageRoll`, `parseSanLoss`; D2 rows 7 and 11). They read the Keeper's actions and the ruleset's own tables, not authored module shapes; whether to consolidate them is Q5.

#### D4.4 Effects: the closed consequence vocabulary

Where a shape states what follows (a result level, a push, a hazard with no roll), it lists effects. Each effect is exactly the argument of one existing verb; the shape supplies the amount, and **the effect is never applied by being read** (P6):

| effect | fields | executed only by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `damage` | the `damage` shape | the Keeper's `apply damage` (`tools.ts:69-70`) |
| `sanity_loss` | the `sanity_loss` shape | the Keeper's `resolve` `sanity:check` (`action.san_loss`, §11.1) |
| `time` | the `time_cost` shape | the Keeper's `apply time` |
| `cost` | the `resource_cost` shape | the engine inside a cast or a combat operation; otherwise the Keeper |
| `flag` | `{flag_id, value}` (the §134.4 semantic id) | the Keeper's `apply flag`; the clerk only as an obligation's settlement (§134.11) |
| `clock` | `{clock_id, ticks}` | the Keeper's `apply threat` |
| `next_step` | `{step}` (inside a `hazard` only) | the operation that ran the step before it (D6.3) |
| `book` | `{line}` | the Keeper, in the fiction |

Conditions such as an infection's CON loss, ejection, a lost possession have no writer verb, so they are `book` lines. An effect kind is added only with its writer.

#### D4.5 The shapes

Common to every shape: closed keys (unknown refused), one optional `book`, references are graph node ids of the kinds named, skill and characteristic names resolve in the ruleset's tables by normalised-name match (`normalize`, `kernel-ts/read/values.ts:100`, the one name matching the repo allows; the tables the SO-03 branch threads into the reader's contract as `contract.rules`), dice per D4.3.

**1. `check` — a stated roll.** The §134.2 declaration form, with a third owner `rule` beside `mod` and `obligation`:

```
{scope: "actor" | "actor-target" | "opposed", target?: <npc|creature id>,
 values: [{path: "skills.<name>" | "characteristics.<X>", label, minimum?}] | approaches_unstated: true,
 selection: "maximum" | "approach",
 opposing?: {values: [...], selection: "maximum"},            // scope opposed only
 difficulty: "regular" | "hard" | "extreme" | difficulty_unstated: true,
 results?: {<critical|extreme|hard|regular|failure|fumble>: {effects?: [effect], book?}},   // rule owner: any subset of the six
 push?: {allowed: boolean, effects?: [effect], book?}}
```

Reuses: the Mod/obligation validator `checkDeclarationRefusals` (`kernel-ts/modules/obligation-shape.ts:45`); the decisions `core-check:ordinary-check` (one value, or `maximum` over several: "DEX or Climb" is one roll against the higher, as the Mod path and SO-05's stairs row already treat it) and `core-check:opposed-check`; the Luck roll is `characteristics.Luck` (the ruleset spells it so, `characteristic-dice.json`) through `push-luck:luck-roll`. The only difference by owner is the result entry (Mod: its own mapping; obligation: `{settles, book}`; rule: `{effects, book}`) and `results` completeness (Mod and obligation need all six levels; a rule states only the levels the book states). Not added: a `combined_all` selection (no module in the inventory states "both must pass"), concealment (the resolve input has no concealed flag outside psychology). Reader: `resolve` with `action.rule` (D6.3); the capsule `mech` line (D6.1); the ordinary-check binder when the player declared it (D8). Who acts: the Keeper, or the clerk under (c) when the player declared that very check (D8).

**2. `damage` — hit points lost to something other than an attack.** `{dice | dice_unstated: true}`. Reuses `apply damage {dice}` (`tools.ts:69-70`). Not added: armour interaction (the rulebook's other damage ignores armour, `hazards.json` note) and severity presets (choosing "severe" from "a fall onto concrete" is a semantic reading; D7). Reader: `apply damage` with `stated` (D6.3); `mech` line. Who acts: the Keeper.

**3. `sanity_loss` — Sanity lost on success / failure.** `{success: <SAN>, failure: <SAN>}`, each half validated by D4.3's sanity rule; `success_unstated`/`failure_unstated` for a page that gives one side. Reuses the `san_loss` string `"S/F"` the sanity engine parses (§11.1, `sanity/index.ts:173-186`): the kernel builds `"S/F"` from the shape; it never searches prose for it. Seats: a rule or hazard; an object or artifact (seeing the floating knife); inside `stat_block` (seeing the actor). Reader: `sanity:check` reads the target actor's `profile.sanity_loss` before its legacy keys, and `action.rule` naming a rule with this shape fills `san_loss`. Who acts: the Keeper (sanity is a consequence; the involuntary behaviour stays the Keeper's pick).

**4. `time_cost` — clock time a stated action takes.** `{amount: <int|DICE>, unit: "round" | "minute" | "hour" | "day" | "week", minimum?: true} | {amount_unstated: true}`. The machine derives minutes for the four clock units (1, 60, 1440, 10080); a `round` is a combat round and never advances the clock. A page that states a span with no count of a unit ("each half-day", "an afternoon") is `amount_unstated` with the span as `book` (Q3). Reuses `apply time {minutes}` (`kernel-ts/apply/index.ts`, `advanceClock` `kernel-ts/apply/clock.ts:36`). Reader: `apply time` with `stated`; `mech` line; a spell's `casting_time`/`duration` (shape 12); a tome's reading times (shape 13). Who acts: the Keeper (SO Q4).

**5. `resource_cost` — magic points, POW, Sanity, Luck or hit points spent.** `{resource: "mp" | "pow" | "san" | "luck" | "hp", amount: <int|DICE> | amount_unstated: true | chosen: true, per?: "use" | "round" | "cast"}`. `chosen: true` records that the book lets the spender choose the amount (Flesh Ward's "variable magic points"), so no number is invented and the engine does not read the word "variable" (`magic/engine.ts:45`). Money is not here: a stated fee or price is `reward.cash` or §58's catalog price. Reuses the spell parameters `cost_mp`/`cost_sanity`/`cost_pow` and the combat operation's `cost` (`exe:140-161`, `exe:364-369`). Reader: through `spell` and `weapon`; the `mech` line. Who acts: the engine within a cast or an operation, otherwise the Keeper.

**6. `gate` — a condition (registered seat).** Exactly the objects `conditionStatus` reads (`mg:107-121`): `{kind: "always"}`, `{kind: "clue_discovered", clue_id}`, `{kind: "flag_set", flag_id, value?}`. Not authorable: a bare string and the text-search fallback (`mg:122-124`), which looks for flag names inside prose. Used inside shapes as `when` (a hazard, a reward) and on routes. Reader: `conditionStatus`/`describeCondition`, the exits' `unlock_when.met`. Who acts: nobody; it is information, and a gate never refuses the Keeper (`kernel-ts/read/assemble.ts:29`, P8).

**7. `hazard` — a danger the book states, the Keeper triggers.**

```
{trigger: {kind: "keeper"} | {kind: "enter"} | {kind: "attempt", guards: {clues?, exits?, people?}},
 when?: <gate>,
 steps?: [<check>, ...],        // ordered; a step's results name the next with {kind: "next_step", step}
 effects?: [effect],            // what happens with no roll

```

Examples in the shape: the chapel floor is `steps: [Luck {failure: [next_step 1]}, Jump {failure: [damage 1D6]}]`; the bed attack `steps: [Spot Hidden {failure: [next_step 1]}, Dodge {failure: [damage 1D6+2]}]` with the witnesses' loss as the rule's own `sanity_loss`. An explicit no-roll (the tingling foreheads near the chapel symbol) is not a hazard: it stays where the reader prompt already puts it (a clue's `delivery_kind: obvious`, or the rule node's wording), and the reviewer still checks it as source information. `trigger` is information for the Keeper ("stated on entry", "stated when they try to reach X"); `attempt` reuses §134.1's guards. Reuses: `check`, the effects, `gate`. Reader: the `mech` line; `resolve` with `action.rule` and `action.step` (D6.3). Never a candidate (D8). Who acts: the Keeper, dice included (SO Q1; Q1 below asks only whether the kernel may roll a step once the Keeper names it — it may not before).

**8. `obligation` — a demand before the investigators get something (registered, §134).** Unchanged. Its check step is shape 1 with owner `obligation`.

**9. `stat_block` — an actor's numbers (registered seat `mechanics.profile`).** Closed keys: `characteristics` {STR, CON, SIZ, DEX, APP, INT, POW, EDU: int}, `derived` {HP, MP, MOV, Build, SAN: int; DB: a ruleset damage-bonus value}, `skills` {name: int}, `weapons` [weapon], `armor` int | `armor_unstated`, `armor_rule` (the engine's closed value `degrades_1_per_damage`, `engine.ts:449`), `spells` [spell name or spell node id], `sanity_loss` (shape 3), and the three provenance keys the starter already writes (`profile_kind`, `characteristic_scale`, `authority`). Removed from the authoring form: `attacks` (prose; an attack is a weapon), `attacks_per_round` (a weapon's `uses_per_round`), `san_loss_to_see` (shape 3); on starters the validator tolerates these three as a listed legacy allowance that shrinks as each starter migrates (the haunting, then `mystery-house`), and the reader's drafts get no allowance. Reuses `npcCombatParticipant` (`prof:86-102`) exactly. Reader: `npcProfileOf` extended to `creature` nodes (today `npc` only, `kernel-ts/resolve/context.ts:44`), the combat, chase, healing and sanity paths that already call it. Who acts: the engine; the clerk under `session_step`.

**10. `weapon` — a weapon an actor or object carries.** The weapon-parameter shape Mod definitions already use (`kernel-ts/mods/definition.ts:92-103`) — `{name, skill, damage, uses_per_round, impale, adds_damage_bonus, base_range_yards?, magazine?, malfunction?}` — or `{extends: <weapons.json id>, name?, …overrides}` as starter profiles write today (`combat/catalog.ts:35-43` merges it). `skill` resolves in the ruleset skill table (the check `kernel-ts/mods/jobs.ts:325-329` makes for usages). ### D6. Readers, seats and operations

**D6.1 One read method, one Keeper line.** `ModuleGraph.mechanicsOf(node)` returns the node's shapes from `recordOf(node).mechanics` plus its registered-seat bridges (spell flat keys), and nothing else reads `mechanics`. The capsule's `where.rules` rows (`sceneRules`, `mg:554-556`, `uses-rule` from the active scene to any node) gain `mech`: one English line rendered by code from the typed values only — e.g. `hazard (Keeper triggers): Luck; on failure Jump; on failure damage 1D6` — at most 160 characters in the compact capsule with `mech_truncated` like `line`. A row whose node has no shapes has no `mech` key, so a module without shapes reads byte for byte as before. §13.2's source table gains the row; `look focus=scene` shows the full line.

**D6.2 Engine inputs.** `npcProfileOf` reads `creature` nodes too; `sanity:check` reads `profile.sanity_loss` first; `moduleSpellRecords` reads `mechanics.spell`; `moduleWeapons` reads graph weapon shapes; `magicLearningSources` reads `mechanics.tome.spells`; the module lookup's `endings[]` and the development binder read `reward`; the threat pressure row reads `clock.advances_on`; SL-07 reads `tactic`.

**D6.3 Operations name a shape; the kernel fills its numbers.**

- `resolve` accepts `action.rule: <handle>` (a `rule`, `hazard` or `tome` node whose `mechanics` holds a `check` or a `hazard`) and, for a hazard, `action.step: <n>` (default 0). The node must be linked from the active scene by `uses-rule`, or be a tome or object present or held. The kernel binds the check exactly as §134.11 binds an obligation's (`kernel-ts/resolve/obligation.ts`: approach among `values`, the higher value for `maximum`, the minimum, the stated difficulty — the Keeper's own when it is unstated — the target present), runs the decision the check maps to, and returns `stated: {rule, step?, level, effects: [<bound effect arguments>], next_step?, book?}`. It writes nothing beyond the roll's own receipts; the receipt carries `basis: {rule}`. Refusals mirror §134.11 (`rule_unknown`, `rule_not_here`, `rule_no_check`, `rule_step`, `rule_skill`, `rule_minimum`, `rule_target`, `rule_difficulty`).
- `apply damage`, `apply time`, `apply threat`, `apply flag` and `apply cash` accept `stated: <handle>`: the amount comes from the stated effect of that kind — the level this turn's `action.rule` result for that node reached, else the node's own shape of that kind (`damage`, `time_cost`, `reward.cash`, `clock.advances_on`). Giving both `stated` and an amount is refused (`stated_conflict`); two candidate effects is refused (`stated_ambiguous`) with the choices. An `apply` without `stated` is exactly today's `apply`: the Keeper's own amount, `basis: keeper` (P8).
- `sanity:check` with `action.rule` naming a node with `sanity_loss` builds `san_loss` from it.

**D6.4 The three ends (§31) for every shape.** *Writer:* the starter author and the reader under review (never the kernel, never a lane). *Reader:* D6.1–D6.3. *Actor:* the Keeper through the `mech` line and `stated`/`action.rule`; the clerk only where D8 says. The offer ledger registers `stated:<handle>` for each `where.rules` row with a `mech` line and marks it taken when a receipt of the turn carries `basis: {rule: <handle>}` or `stated: <handle>`; `kpi.py` counts it under its own kind. It counts and never nags (§31.2).

### D7. The PDF path ("数值化方案")

**What the reader writes.** A mechanical statement goes into `properties.mechanics.<shape>` of the node that states it, beside its wording in `summary`; the reader writes no ad-hoc mechanical key. A rule node is `{node_kind: "rule", name, summary: <the book's wording, abridged>, source_refs, properties: {mechanics: {…}}}`, linked from its scene by `uses-rule` exactly as today; an obligation is still a `requirement` node with its `calls-for-check` claim to the rule node holding the wording (SO-03). An actor's numbers stay in `mechanics.profile` (reader lines 102-104), now with `weapons` as weapon shapes, `sanity_loss` as a shape and `tactic` when the book states one; the same for a `creature`.

**One instruction per shape in `content/setup/visual-reader.md`** (English, no book- or language-specific wording), in the read phase after SO-03's obligation paragraph: the D4.1 container, then one short paragraph per shape giving its keys and one worked example, the D4.2 rule ("record what the page does not state as `<slot>_unstated`; never fill it"), and D4.3's dice rule ("dice are `1D4+2`, never `1D4+2 hit points`; damage bonus is `adds_damage_bonus`"). The reviewer's section gains one paragraph: every field under `mechanics` is a mechanical statement to check against the page image, including that an `_unstated` flag is true (the page really does not state it) and that the shape sits on the right node (the stairs' check on the stairs, not on the pit).

**Critical and review.** `shapeReviewPaths(node, base)` — generalising SO-03's `obligationReviewPaths` (`kernel-ts/modules/obligation-review.ts` on the SO-03 branch), one import-free file loaded by both the kernel's `checkDraft` and the extension's `reviewUnits` — lists every leaf under `mechanics`, strings included, so dice strings enter `required_review` whether or not the reader put them in `critical`. Numbers keep entering through `numericPaths` (`visual.ts:38-46`); the two sets are unioned. A review that leaves any of them unsupported fails publication (`checkReview`, `visual.ts:280-282`), and a repair has the §74 choice: cite a page that prints the value, or delete it — or, for a shape, mark the slot `_unstated`.

**The draft checker runs the shared validator.** `checkDraft` calls `mechanicsRefusals` over the overlay of known and drafted nodes and claims (SO-03's `checkObligations` construction), with the ruleset's names from `contract.rules`, and refuses with node, pointer and rule; its `fix` says to record an unstated slot as unstated, to name skills as `details.ruleset` spells them, and to delete a shape the page does not state. The NPC-numbers check at `visual.ts:157-158` extends to flat characteristic integers on `npc` and `creature` nodes (the ruleset's closed characteristic names), so the twin's flat `STR: 90` form cannot be published again.

**Existing PDF-built modules.** Shapes arrive only through the detail request path with an explicit question (§22, "no all-book reparsing", `docs/kernel-rpc.md:2655-2657`), reviewed additively; an already-ready rule node receives `mechanics` as a delta under the existing merge rules (`mergeValue`, `visual.ts:84`). No backfill job, no migration of a published graph by code, and the committed twin is never rebuilt (it is the control, D9).

**What the reader must not do:** guess a difficulty ("Obscure Clue" is not `hard` unless the page says so; the flag is `difficulty_unstated`); fill a Keeper's choice (Flesh Ward's magic points are `chosen`); turn a span into minutes ("each half-day" is `amount_unstated`, Q3); pick a severity for an unstated fall; convert editions or recompute printed NPC values (reader line 106); copy rulebook defaults into a module shape; write a shape the page does not state to make a node look complete; and on the host side, no code classifies rule prose into shapes, and no gate counts shapes (a book may state none).

### D10. Interaction with existing rules

- **§134 and SO-04.** The obligation's check step is shape 1; `action.rule` reuses `bindObligation`'s validation, not a copy. A rule node's check that guards what the player seeks is an obligation, not a rule check: the validator's `shape_duplicate` keeps one owner.
- **SL-07.** Owns `tactic`'s key, reader and receipt. This spec's only ask is the seat (D4.5, shape 11).
- **§32 admission.** An operation with `action.rule` or `stated` is an ordinary Keeper operation and passes admission as today; policy-origin ones follow SL-02's measurement (§135.10). No exemption is granted here.
- **§36 adaptation.** An adapted scene does not inherit its source scene's `uses-rule` shapes (the SO precedent); `add_npc` keeps "no model numeric mechanics"; an adaptation never writes `mechanics`.
- **§22.** Additive detail only; no reparse; the kernel still never parses a PDF.
- **§102 combat operations.** The starter's `rules_operation` stays the registered seat of a scripted encounter. A PDF book has no way to state one; its Corbitt fights with his stat block and the Keeper's rulings. Typing encounter scripts for PDF books is out of scope (the owner's list does not name it, and it is the largest shape).
- **Mods.** One check form; Mod bytes are frozen per version, so `natural-npc` 1.4.2 loads unchanged. `enhanced-items` generates weapon, spell and usage definitions at run time through `definition.ts`/`usages.ts`; the catalog reuses those shapes and does not touch that path. A Mod contribution kind for stated mechanics (`contributes.mechanics`) is not added: no shipped Mod states one (every numeric key in `mods/` is a setting or a prompt).
- **§16.2 mechanics projection.** Receipts and their rows are unchanged; `basis` is Keeper-side.
- **Worldlines, recovery, history.** Shapes are read-only graph data; the receipts that used them fork, replay and roll back as receipts do.
- **§11.5.1 (the investigator's standing defence, another session).** Not touched.

## Testing Decisions

A good test drives the real entry and asserts what the kernel issued, what receipts exist and what the Keeper and Jev were shown — never private state or call order. Every product-behaviour test must be killed by a mutation of the code it covers (a named mutation per rule, recorded in the ticket's report), and no test may pass through a helper that normalises its input first (memory: "tests must travel the real path", "a test helper can hide the defect").

- **Validator, through the real entries.** Starter registration (`module.register` over a derived content root whose haunting graph carries the case), the reader's `checkDraft` (a draft through the offline check, as SO-03's `tests/extension/obligation-reader.test.mjs` does), and `mods.install` of a package derived from `natural-npc`. One case per D5 rule, each refused by its `rule` and each shown to go green when its rule is removed from the validator; accepted: the minimal node of every shape, an `_unstated` variant of every needable slot, the haunting after migration. Prior art: `tests/extension/obligation-shape.test.mjs` (§134.8).
- **The dice grammar.** A table test that every string `definitionExpression` accepts is rolled by `rollExpression` and by `CombatSession.rollDamageExpression`, and that `1D4+2 hit points`, `1D6+DB`, `½DB`, `2D6 armor` are refused with `shape_dice`.
- **Kernel readers (pytest over the emitted `build/kernel/rpc.mjs`; build first; one pytest at a time, on the ticket's own worktree).** On a fresh haunting campaign after the migration ticket, seeded: the chapel's `where.rules` row carries the `mech` line; `resolve` with `action.rule: chapel-floor-collapse` binds Luck, a forced failure returns `next_step: 1`, `action.step: 1` binds Jump, a forced failure returns the bound `damage 1D6` and writes no HP; `apply damage {stated: chapel-floor-collapse}` rolls `1D6` with `basis: stated`, and the same `apply` with `dice` too is refused `stated_conflict`; `apply damage {dice: "2D6"}` without `stated` lands with `basis: keeper` (the module is reference); `sanity:check` targeting Corbitt uses `1/1D8` from the typed profile; `development:end-session` without a reward expression uses the ending's `1D6`; a `creature` with a stat block can be fought. Prior art: `tests/kernel/test_scene_obligations.py`, `tests/kernel/test_sessions.py`.
- **Controls (goldens).** Capsule, `table.apply.options`, `table.resolve.options` and the module lookup of `mystery-house`, `voice-bench` and `the-haunting-rulebook`, walked scene by scene, byte-identical to goldens recorded on each ticket's parent commit (SO-02's method). The haunting differs only at the migrated scenes and its ending row; the diff is reviewed row by row, not re-recorded wholesale.
- **The reader (no committed book text).** Checker tests for a draft with a prose dice string, an unviewed page, an unknown skill, both a value and its `_unstated` flag, a shape on the wrong kind; a valid draft whose shape fields are not in `critical` gets every leaf in `required_review`, dice strings included (the `numericPaths` gap of D2). A detail read on a scratch copy of a book the owner chooses, with a question that reaches a stated hazard and an NPC's stat block, produces reviewed shapes; the evidence stays in the scratch workspace; the committed twin is not rebuilt.
- **Jev seams (stub ports, SL-02's policy tests).** A declared check reaching a clue with a stated gate arrives with skill and difficulty bound and basis the clue; a hazard, a damage, a sanity loss and a cost never become candidates; a stated check with an unstated difficulty goes to `infer(bind)`; a `minimum` the actor lacks withholds the candidate.
- **The replay (after SL-02 and SO-04 are on the branch).** A pre-registered replay on a recorded live haunting turn that reaches a stated hazard (the chapel floor; recorded at a real table, never synthesised — if no such turn exists, the live gate below records it first), outcomes written before the run: the Keeper's damage operation carries `stated` (or the Keeper improvises with `basis: keeper`, reported as such, never as a pass or a failure); no clerk step applies a consequence; the turn's LLM step count is recorded against the same turn on the parent (no saving is promised: the hazard is the Keeper's by ruling).
- **Live gate.** A real table (the Keeper model the owner fixes for acceptance, the main session as the one player, one sentence a turn, `tests/play/driver.py`) that reaches the chapel and the basement after the migration, at the single-loop plan's SL-05 gate. Not a worker ticket; no scripted player, no batch settle.
- **Baselines stay green.** `npm run test:ext`; `uv run --frozen python -m pytest tests/kernel tests/play` after `build:runtime`, never two at once, never while the tree changes; the pi-backend vitest judged by failing names.

## Out of Scope

- A tome-reading runtime (Mythos gain and Sanity applied by the kernel on a read), a disease or characteristic-loss runtime, a healing shape.
- Typed encounter scripts (`rules_operation`) for PDF books; the starter's stays the registered seat.

## Further Notes

**Terms proposed for `CONTEXT.md`** (not edited here; candidates for `/domain-modeling` once accepted):
- *mechanical shape* — a typed value from the closed catalog that states a rule the module prints (a check, a die, a quantity, a condition, an actor's numbers); it fixes how much, never who decides whether.
- *stated parameter* — a value an operation takes from a mechanical shape instead of from the model (`action.rule`, `apply … {stated}`); its receipt says `basis: stated`.
- *shape seat* — the one place a shape lives on its node (`mechanics.<shape>` of the record view, or a registered seat); a second copy is refused.

**Contract.** When implemented, the catalog lands as a new `docs/kernel-rpc.md` section at the next free number (§133–§135 are taken on the branches that exist today, and SL-07 may take the next); it amends §13.2 (the `mech` row), §22 (the reader's shapes and review paths), §26/§134.2–§134.3 (the `rule` owner of the check form), §11 (`action.rule`), §5 (`apply … {stated}`), §31 (the `stated:` offer kind) and §32.5 (the clock's `advances_on` answers its open question).
