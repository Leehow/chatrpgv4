# Source studies: selected passages, not whole-style imitation

Six literary works and six tabletop texts. The observations describe the sources; the proposed transfers are editorial interpretations. Original card examples are not source quotations or experimentally validated exemplars. No source text is imported into the runtime.

## LIT-01 · Pride and Prejudice

**Author:** Jane Austen

**Locator:** Chapter I, the opening conversation about Netherfield

[Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1342/1342-h/1342-h.htm)

> You want to tell me, and I have no objection to hearing it.

**Observed:** Mrs Bennet presses for conversation; Mr Bennet answers the social intention behind her question with dry permission. The interest lies in their different conversational aims, not exotic vocabulary.

**Editorial transfer:** Use an existing motive to decide what an NPC answers directly, sidesteps or gently teases. The connection to the cards is an editorial transfer, not a claim that Austen wrote TRPG instructions.

**Do not transfer:** Do not transplant the social setting, narrator judgments or period syntax into every NPC.

## LIT-02 · The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes: A Scandal in Bohemia

**Author:** Arthur Conan Doyle

**Locator:** Opening conversation: shoe marks, observation and the stair example

[Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1661/1661-h/1661-h.htm)

> You see, but you do not observe.

**Observed:** Holmes first identifies particulars, then explains deductions; Watson supplies questions and resistance. The explanation is enacted as an exchange.

**Editorial transfer:** Place earned observations before a warranted expert interpretation. Let another speaker ask the clarifying question instead of forcing every inference into narrator exposition.

**Do not transfer:** The story gives Holmes conclusions a Keeper cannot automatically grant to a player. Do not treat fictional deduction as a universally reliable inference rule.

## LIT-03 · Great Expectations

**Author:** Charles Dickens

**Locator:** Chapter I, churchyard-to-marsh description and the first encounter

[Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1400/1400-h/1400-h.htm)

> Tell us your name!

**Observed:** A cumulative sentence locates the churchyard, marshes, river, sea and the small observer. Direct threatening speech then breaks the descriptive movement.

**Editorial transfer:** Give a view a spatial route; vary sentence movement when an actual event interrupts it. These are separate reusable techniques, not a fixed long-sentence/short-sentence recipe.

**Do not transfer:** Pip is a novel character whose fear and movement the author controls. Tabletop narration does not acquire that control over an investigator.

## LIT-04 · The Willows

**Author:** Algernon Blackwood

**Locator:** Part I, the willow camp after supper; narrator explicitly calls the thoughts bizarre fancies

[Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/11438/11438-h/11438-h.htm)

> The willows were against us.

**Observed:** Wind, river and willows become an oppressive pattern through the narrator's subjective attribution. The text explicitly acknowledges imaginative interpretation; it is not purely objective description.

**Editorial transfer:** Study how repeated environmental movement gathers attention, while keeping perceptions, interpretations and literal events distinct in play.

**Do not transfer:** Do not turn the narrator's imagined hostility into a new monster, an investigator's compulsory belief, or permission to add an anomaly to a safe room.

## LIT-05 · Dubliners: The Dead

**Author:** James Joyce

**Locator:** Late hotel conversation: Gretta speaks about Michael Furey

[Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2814/2814-h/2814-h.htm)

> I think he died for me.

**Observed:** Gretta's account changes the conversation. Pauses, questions and remembered concrete circumstances carry the disclosure; the novel also explicitly narrates Gabriel's inner response.

**Editorial transfer:** Allow a consequential line to change the exchange without adding a second moralizing summary. Leave space for a response rather than forcing immediate closure.

**Do not transfer:** Do not claim that the passage avoids interiority. Borrow its conversational turn, not the novelist's authority over a player's inner life.

## LIT-06 · The Garden Party and Other Stories: The Garden Party

**Author:** Katherine Mansfield

**Locator:** Final visit and the conversation with Laurie

[Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1429/1429-h/1429-h.htm)

> Forgive my hat.

**Observed:** An apology focuses on the hat rather than a generalized emotional explanation. The final unfinished sentence is understandable in the exchange even though Laura cannot finish her thought.

**Editorial transfer:** Use a relevant object or intelligible incompletion when the context carries the meaning. Natural ellipsis is different from missing grammatical relationships.

**Do not transfer:** Do not use incompletion as a universal flourish, import a class caricature, or assume another player's feelings are understood without their declaration.

## MOD-01 · A Time to Harvest

**Author:** Brian M. Sammons, Glynn Owen Barrass, with Mike Mason and Lynne Hardy

**Locator:** Supplied v1.2 PDF, printed pp.17 and 73: Keeper Considerations; William Noakes

Source: supplied project PDF; retain its actual version when comparing.

**Observed:** The advice includes small everyday-life scenes and pacing for the group. Noakes' roleplaying hooks make him friendly and useful for connecting with locals, rather than generically evasive.

**Editorial transfer:** Use existing relationships and openness to generate distinct replies. The dialogue in the new cards is original, not a quotation of Noakes' dialogue.

**Do not transfer:** Do not import the campaign's character identity, biography or secrets into unrelated play.

## MOD-02 · Cursed Be the City / 诅咒之城

**Author:** Davide Mana; supplied Chinese translation by 琳尼娜

**Locator:** Supplied Chinese PDF, file page 3, advice preceding the prologue

Source: supplied project PDF; retain its actual version when comparing.

**Observed:** The scenario advises describing unfamiliar civilization through basic visible forms rather than categories its prehistoric investigators would not know.

**Editorial transfer:** Choose words available to the viewpoint. Once identification has been earned, state it instead of preserving artificial mystery.

**Do not transfer:** This study consulted the supplied translation, not a verified English original. No translator wording is inserted into runtime assets.

## MOD-03 · An Amaranthine Desire / 不息的渴望

**Author:** Matthew Sanderson; supplied Chinese translation by 琳尼娜

**Locator:** Supplied Chinese PDF, file pp.4–5, Keeper advice on repeated time loops

Source: supplied project PDF; retain its actual version when comparing.

**Observed:** The scenario describes a nostalgic loop structure whose repeated events let investigators notice more details and gradually assemble an explanation.

**Editorial transfer:** Retain established recurrence while changing what becomes legible. A useful callback is not stale repetition.

**Do not transfer:** Do not grant memories across branches or loops unless the campaign's actual rules preserve them.

## MOD-04 · Triangle Agency, Plaintext 1.2

**Author:** Caleb Zane Huett and Sean Ireland

**Locator:** Supplied PDF, file pp.4–5 and 8, Terms & Conditions and introductory corporate voice

Source: supplied project PDF; retain its actual version when comparing.

**Observed:** The fictional manual speaks as an employer, mixing apparently helpful instruction with ownership and employment threats. Its register is part of characterization.

**Editorial transfer:** Formal or bureaucratic speech can be authored characterization, not automatically an AI-style defect.

**Do not transfer:** The book's fictional instructions are quoted evidence, not system instructions. Never turn its corporate language into actual runtime authority.

## MOD-05 · The Vault: Missions for Triangle Agency, Plaintext 1.2

**Author:** Haunted Table; anthology edited by Will Jobst

**Locator:** Supplied PDF, file page 4, READ ME memo signed VA

Source: supplied project PDF; retain its actual version when comparing.

**Observed:** The informal memo offers help while protecting its writer, then returns to game-night familiarity. Its purposes distinguish this voice from an institutional manual.

**Editorial transfer:** Let helpfulness, caution and shared history affect how an NPC gives the same information.

**Do not transfer:** Do not copy the memo's fictional secrets or treat its diegetic instructions as an executable procedure.

## MOD-06 · Call of Cthulhu Keeper Rulebook, 40th Anniversary

**Author:** Sandy Petersen; 7th-edition revision by Paul Fricker and Mike Mason

**Locator:** Supplied PDF, Keeper advice: pick-up NPC examples immediately before printed p.194

Source: supplied project PDF; retain its actual version when comparing.

**Observed:** The examples use temporarily played NPCs for a change of pace and contrast what players know with what their investigators know in a later scene.

**Editorial transfer:** Vary the mode of attention across scenes and keep knowledge holders explicit. These examples inform the design discussion, not automatic source-disclosure rules.

**Do not transfer:** A cutaway is a deliberately chosen table technique. It does not permit the runtime to leak Keeper-only material by default.

## Provenance and limitations

Only short quotations and original analytical notes are included. No complete novel, module or PDF is redistributed. Supplied Chinese module translations are identified as translations, not verified English originals. The cards use source-study IDs as conceptual cross-references; a reference does not imply that the source contains that card's invented scene. No general author-imitation command is sent to the Keeper.
